📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 7.09 — Major Arteries
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 7.09 and complete the activities in Lesson 7.09, including the full arterial-tree drawing.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
A clinician needs to translate between named pulse points on a body and the arteries underneath. In this assignment you submit your path diagram and your arterial-tree drawing, then identify the artery responsible for each of six common pulse points.
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Submit your arterial-tree path diagram (aorta + arch + paths to fingertip and foot + abdominal branches).
2
Submit a photo (or export) of your Circulatory Map with the arterial tree drawn.
3
Identify the underlying artery at each of these six clinical pulse points: (a) radial pulse at the wrist, (b) carotid pulse in the neck, (c) brachial pulse at the elbow (used in blood-pressure measurement), (d) femoral pulse in the groin, (e) popliteal pulse behind the knee, (f) dorsalis pedis pulse on top of the foot (note: this is a continuation of the anterior tibial). One sentence per pulse.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Path diagram includes aorta + 3 arch branches + both limb paths + 4 abdominal branches with what each supplies.
☐
Circulatory Map shows the arterial tree in correct positions.
☐
All six pulse-point arteries correctly identified.
