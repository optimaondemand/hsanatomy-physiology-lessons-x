Venous return path diagram
15 min
1
Draw the venous return from a fingertip to the right atrium and from a toe to the right atrium. Label every named vein.
2
In 2–3 sentences, explain the hepatic portal detour: blood draining the gut travels through the portal vein to the liver before rejoining general circulation via the hepatic veins and IVC. Why would the body route gut-drained blood through the liver first, rather than straight back to the heart?
3
Sketch the skeletal-muscle pump
10 min
1
Cover Figure 20.15 in the reading and sketch a leg vein with two valves — one open, one closed — and surrounding muscle, from memory. Show what happens when the muscle contracts.
2
Add an arrow showing the direction blood is pushed, then write one sentence on why the valve, not just the muscle squeeze, is what actually stops blood from sliding back down.
3
Now check your sketch against Figure 20.15 and correct anything you got wrong.
✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK
What to draw. Add the venous return to the Circulatory Map you built last lesson. Color all of this in blue.
• Head/neck — internal jugular + external jugular (both sides).
• Upper limb (both sides) — deep: subclavian, axillary; superficial: cephalic, basilic, median cubital (highlight at the elbow for phlebotomy).
• Brachiocephalic veins joining to form the SVC.
• Lower limb (both sides) — deep: popliteal → femoral → external iliac; superficial: the long great saphenous vein running up the medial leg.
• Common iliac veins joining to form the IVC.
• Portal vein — a separate detour from the gut to the liver before merging back into the IVC. Sets up Module 10 (Digestive).
