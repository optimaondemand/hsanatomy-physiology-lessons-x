Lesson 7.12
Fetal Circulation and Changes at Birth
Lesson
Module thread — Service. The fetal circulation gives the still-developing fetus everything it cannot yet provide for itself. The first breath rearranges the entire system in seconds.
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Reading 7.12 — Fetal Circulation
Before you start the activities below, read Reading 7.12 end-to-end.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name and place the three fetal shunts (ductus venosus, foramen ovale, ductus arteriosus) and what each bypasses.
2
I will be able to describe what happens at the first breath and trace the mechanism by which each shunt closes.
3
I will be able to name the adult anatomical landmarks left behind by the closed shunts (fossa ovalis, ligamentum arteriosum).

✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK
What to do. Two tasks today — draw the fetal shunts, and assemble for the module-end Assembled Figure.
Part 1 — Draw the fetal shunts (detail page). On a fresh page, draw a small simplified fetal heart-plus-placenta diagram showing:
• Umbilical vein (oxygenated, color red) and two umbilical arteries (deoxygenated, color blue).
• Ductus venosus — from umbilical vein into IVC.
• Foramen ovale — opening in the interatrial septum.
• Ductus arteriosus — from pulmonary trunk to aortic arch.
Part 2 — Module-end reassembly. Lay out your Circulatory Map and every detail page on a table or the floor (or on one slide, if you are working digitally). Confirm the heart + great vessels + coronary views + arterial tree + venous tree + red and white blood cell pages + fetal-shunt diagram are all present.
