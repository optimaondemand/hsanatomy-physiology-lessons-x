🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to state Q = ΔP / R and R ∝ 1/r⁴ in my own words, and predict how blood flow changes when one variable changes.

2
Predict the effect on cardiac output
15 min
1
For each scenario below, predict the direction of change (increase / decrease / no change) for the variable in bold, then explain your reasoning in 1–2 sentences using CO = HR × SV or Q = ΔP / R.
2
Scenario A — Valve failure. A narrowed aortic valve (stenosis) restricts how much blood the left ventricle can eject each beat. If HR stays the same, predict what happens to stroke volume and to CO, and name one compensation the heart is likely to attempt.
3
Scenario B — Increased cholesterol. Atherosclerosis narrows the radius of systemic arterioles. Using R ∝ 1/r⁴, predict what happens to total peripheral resistance and to blood pressure if CO stays the same.
4
Scenario C — Decreased blood pressure. Dehydration lowers blood volume and venous return. Predict what happens to preload, and then predict the reflex change in heart rate the body makes to try to keep CO adequate.
💻 YOUR INTERACTIVE SESSION THIS WEEK
No drawing session this lesson. The content here is the physics of fluid flow, not anatomy. The right way to internalize it is to play with pressure and resistance in a simulation where you can watch flow respond.
The Pressure Lab (just below). Use the interactive directly below this box: sliders for heart rate, blood volume and arteriole radius, with live cardiac output, resistance and blood pressure. For a second look from another simulator, the PhET “Blood Pressure Basics” (phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/blood-pressure-basics, free, no login required) is optional.
Time: ~25 minutes — long enough to genuinely play with the parameters.
