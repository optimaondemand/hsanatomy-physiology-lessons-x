Module 7 Writing Assignment
Pressure, Resistance, and the Damage of Hypertension
Analytical Essay · 30 pts · Due end of Module 7 (Week 3)
Module thread — Service. The heart pumps for every other organ. Three weeks of anatomy now bear down on a single physiological question: how does the pump that gives so generously slowly destroy itself when the system it pumps into demands too much pressure?
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
In Module 7 you traced blood from its formed elements through the four chambers, through the great vessels, and out across the entire systemic tree. You met the two equations — Q = ΔP / R and R ∝ 1/r⁴ — that govern the system’s physics, and you saw what happens to specific organs when blood pressure is chronically high. This essay asks you to assemble those pieces into a single argument: how does the heart actually generate pressure, why do arteries, capillaries, and veins handle that pressure differently, and what specifically goes wrong, organ by organ, in chronic hypertension?
✏️ THE PROMPT
Blood flow through the cardiovascular system is governed by physical principles — pressure gradients, vessel diameter, and blood viscosity. Explain how the heart generates pressure, how arteries, capillaries, and veins handle that pressure differently, and what happens to blood flow in chronic hypertension. Reference at least two of the relationships you learned (e.g., Q = ΔP / R, the role of vessel radius to the fourth power). Conclude with one specific way hypertension damages a body organ over time.
Length: 500–700 words.
📝 WHAT A STRONG ESSAY INCLUDES
1
Open with the pump. Sketch where pressure comes from. The left ventricle’s thick myocardium contracts during systole and ejects about 70 mL of blood at ~120 mmHg into the aorta. The elastic recoil of large arteries during diastole maintains pressure even when the ventricle is relaxed (the diastolic ~80 mmHg). State the systolic/diastolic numbers and what they mean physiologically.
2
The two equations. Name and explain Q = ΔP / R (flow equals pressure gradient over resistance) and R ∝ 1/r⁴ (resistance is inversely proportional to the fourth power of vessel radius). Show why the radius rule is so consequential — a small constriction produces a large resistance change.
3
Three vessel types, three pressure handlings. Distinguish the role of large arteries (elastic, smooth the pressure pulse, lose little pressure), arterioles (resistance vessels, where most of the pressure drop occurs, the body’s control valve), capillaries (low-pressure exchange surface, single-cell-thick walls), and veins (capacitance vessels, holding most of the blood volume at low pressure). Make form-follows-function the through-line.
4
What chronic hypertension does. The arterioles are constricted (often from a mix of genetic, dietary, and lifestyle factors); systemic resistance is permanently elevated; the left ventricle has to push harder against that resistance every single beat. Explain how the equations predict that an elevated R, given a roughly stable cardiac output, raises mean arterial pressure throughout the system.
5
One specific organ damage in detail. Conclude by picking one downstream consequence and tracing it concretely: left-ventricular hypertrophy and eventual heart failure (heart that thickened in response to the load), hypertensive stroke (cerebral arterioles that rupture or get atherosclerotic), or hypertensive nephropathy (glomerular damage from chronic high-pressure filtration). Pick one and explain the mechanism.
6
Close with the structure-function frame. The cardiovascular system is built for steady moderate pressure. When pressure is forced upward for years, the system bends, hardens, and eventually breaks where it must — in the heart that pumps it, the brain that filters it, the kidneys that strain it. Tie the closing back to the module’s service thread: the system that gives so generously is the one most punished when asked for too much.

☐
