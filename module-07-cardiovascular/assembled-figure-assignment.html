Module 7 Assembled Figure Assignment
The Assembled Cardiovascular System
Figure submission · 25 pts · Due end of Module 7 (Week 3)
Module thread — Service. The heart that gives, the river that visits every cell. Your figure is three weeks of patient drawing collapsed into a single image — pump, vessels, and the cells they carry.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Over three weeks of this module you have drawn blood (formed elements at exaggerated scale), the heart (external and internal, with the conduction system and coronary tree), and the great arterial and venous trees on your Circulatory Map, a large front-view body outline. For this assignment you gather all of that work into one complete view, photograph (or export) it, and submit it with progress images and a short reflection caption. Paper is highly recommended (draw by hand, lay the pages out together, photograph in good light); a digital drawing assembled on one slide is also accepted. This is the record of having built the cardiovascular system over three weeks — not a perfect textbook diagram, but the figure you actually made.
🧸 EXPECTED STRUCTURES IN YOUR FIGURE
Your figure must show the cardiovascular system on your Circulatory Map plus the required cellular detail pages and heart drawings. Specifically:
The heart — heart drawings
▶
Heart in correct position in the mediastinum, with the apex pointing down and to the left. Pericardium shown as a dashed outline so the heart is visible.
▶
External anatomy: both auricles visible; four chambers in correct spatial arrangement.
▶
Six great vessels: SVC, IVC, pulmonary trunk, four pulmonary veins, aorta — correctly attached and color-coded (blue for deoxygenated path, red for oxygenated path).
▶
Internal heart anatomy (front-cut cross-section drawing): the four chambers, the four valves with visible cusps, the interventricular septum, and the AV-valve chordae tendineae.
▶
Conduction system in a distinct color (yellow recommended): SA node, AV node, bundle of His, bundle branches, Purkinje fibers.
▶
Coronary tree: LAD, circumflex, and right coronary artery, plus the great cardiac vein.
The arterial tree — Circulatory Map
▶
Aorta (ascending, arch, thoracic, abdominal) with the three arch branches in correct order: brachiocephalic, left common carotid, left subclavian.
▶
Head & neck: common carotids (internal + external), vertebrals.
▶
Upper limbs (both): subclavian → axillary → brachial → radial + ulnar.
▶
Abdominal aorta branches: celiac, superior mesenteric, renal arteries (both), inferior mesenteric.
▶
Lower limbs (both): common iliac → external iliac → femoral → popliteal → anterior tibial + posterior tibial + fibular.
The venous return — Circulatory Map
▶
Major veins: internal & external jugulars, brachiocephalics → SVC; subclavian, axillary, basilic, cephalic, median cubital; common iliacs → IVC; femoral, popliteal, great saphenous.
▶
Hepatic portal vein as a separate detour from gut to liver (sets up Module 10).
Cellular detail page (required)
▶
One biconcave-disc red blood cell at exaggerated scale.
▶
The five leukocyte drawings (neutrophil, lymphocyte, monocyte, eosinophil, basophil) in a row alongside the RBC for scale comparison.
Optional detail page
▶
Fetal-shunt diagram (ductus venosus, foramen ovale, ductus arteriosus, umbilical vessels) from Lesson 7.12 — optional but encouraged.
✏️ REFLECTION CAPTION (75–125 words)
Record yourself speaking a caption of 75–125 words (about 45–60 seconds) with your phone, laptop, or the Canvas media recorder; a written caption is accepted only if you genuinely cannot record. Your caption should:
1. Name what you drew (broad regions + your detail page).
2. Identify one substantive thing you learned about how the cardiovascular system works — not just what it looks like. (Example: “Tracing the radial artery from the wrist all the way back to the aortic arch made me appreciate why a pulse in the wrist is a real measurement of left-ventricular function. The brachial pressure cuff is sampling the same arterial wave just one step closer to the heart.”)
Attach three dated progress images — one page from each week (for example, from Lessons 7.02, 7.06, and 7.09).
