Lesson 7.02
Red Blood Cells: Hemoglobin and Oxygen Transport
Lesson
Module thread — Service. The red cell gives up its nucleus, its mitochondria, and its own ability to survive past 120 days — all so that the body can breathe. Notice today the kind of cell built entirely to serve another’s need.
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Reading 7.02 — Red Blood Cells: Hemoglobin and Oxygen Transport
The substantive content for this lesson lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before you start the activities below, open Reading 7.02 in Canvas and read it end-to-end.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to describe at least three features of the red blood cell that all serve the same function (oxygen transport).
2
I will be able to name the four subunits of a hemoglobin molecule, the role of the heme group, and the number of O₂ molecules each hemoglobin can carry.
3
I will be able to explain cooperative binding and why it makes hemoglobin a good loader and unloader of oxygen.

✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK
What to draw. On a fresh page (paper highly recommended), draw a single biconcave-disc red blood cell at an exaggerated scale (roughly 15 cm across — about 20,000× real size). Draw it twice: face-on, and as a side-view cross-section that shows the central dimple deeper than the rim. Color it the bright red of oxygenated hemoglobin.
Optional detail. If you have time, draw a smaller cluster beside the cell as a stylized hemoglobin tetramer (four subunits, four heme groups). You will not get the protein folding right — nobody does in clay or in pencil — but a four-blob cluster captures the idea.
Goal. Look at the surface area you have drawn, and compare the cell’s thickness to its diameter in your side view — it really is much wider than it is tall. This is the cell that will be carrying every breath you take in Module 9.
