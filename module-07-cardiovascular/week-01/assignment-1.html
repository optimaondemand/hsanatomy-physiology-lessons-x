Lesson focus: What Is Blood? Plasma and the Formed Elements
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
When a blood sample is spun in a centrifuge, the contents separate by density into clearly visible layers: plasma at the top, a thin white-ish band called the buffy coat in the middle, and packed red blood cells at the bottom. The proportions of those layers are the easiest visual representation of what blood is. In this assignment you submit a carefully proportioned, fully labeled centrifuge-tube diagram, together with a short commentary explaining what each layer carries and why the proportions are what they are.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If you doubled a person’s hematocrit, what would change about how blood does its job — both for the better and for the worse?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Draw a centrifuge-tube diagram, scaled approximately to the real proportions: ~55% plasma on top, <1% buffy coat in the middle, ~45% packed red blood cells at the bottom. Label the layers and the hematocrit line.
2
Annotate the plasma layer with the three plasma-protein families (albumin, globulins, fibrinogen) and one specific job each does.
3
Annotate the buffy coat with the two cellular populations it contains (leukocytes + platelets), and the red cell layer with the rough count of cells per microliter.
4
Below the diagram, write a 200–300 word commentary. Engage the focus question in a final paragraph — what would change, for the better and for the worse, if hematocrit doubled?
5
Submit your diagram (photo, scan, or digital drawing) plus your commentary as a single .pdf or .docx, or paste an image of the diagram into a Canvas text entry with your commentary below it.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Diagram is reasonably proportioned (plasma roughly 55%, RBCs roughly 45%, buffy coat is a thin line).
☐
All three plasma proteins named, each tied to a specific function.
☐
Buffy coat and RBC layer labeled with correct cell populations.
☐
Commentary engages the focus question with a specific gain and a specific loss (viscosity, oxygen carrying, clot risk are all good directions).
