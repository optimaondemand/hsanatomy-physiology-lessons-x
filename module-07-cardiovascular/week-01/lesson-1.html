Reading 7.01 — What Is Blood? Plasma and the Formed Elements
The substantive content for this lesson lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before you start the activities below, open Reading 7.01 in Canvas and read it end-to-end. The activities on this page assume you have done so.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to describe blood as a connective tissue and explain why a liquid counts as a tissue.
2
I will be able to name the rough percentage of blood that is plasma versus formed elements, and the three plasma-protein families.
3
I will be able to name the three families of formed elements and state which is most abundant by count.

⚡ TODAY’S ACTIVITIES (85 minutes total)
1
Read Reading 7.01
22 min
1
Open Reading 7.01 in Canvas. Read it end-to-end, including the inset on William Harvey.
2
Try the self-check accordion before clicking it. Name the rough plasma/formed-element split and the dominant formed element by count.
2
Recorded narration
15 min
1
Close Reading 7.01. Jot 4–5 bullet points from memory, then record yourself narrating what you just read in 60–90 seconds from those bullets — don’t write out or read a full script.
2
Re-open the reading and listen back. What did you remember? What did you miss? Note the corrections — you don’t need to re-record.
3
Centrifuge-tube diagram (the goal of today’s assignment)
15 min
1
On a clean page, draw a tall, narrow test tube. Imagine you have just spun a blood sample in a centrifuge: the heaviest elements drop to the bottom, the lightest stay on top.
2
Divide your tube into three horizontal bands at the correct proportions: about 55% plasma on top (label the three plasma-protein families on the side), a thin buffy-coat layer in the middle (label: leukocytes + platelets, <1%), and about 45% packed red blood cells at the bottom (label: hematocrit).
3
Beside your diagram, write one sentence explaining what role each layer plays in keeping you alive. This diagram becomes the figure you submit with your assignment.
🥽 YOUR VR SESSION THIS WEEK
No VR sculpting this lesson. The components of blood are microscopic, and the right way to meet them for the first time is on a screen where their relative proportions can be drawn to scale. Sculpting begins in Lesson 7.02 with the red blood cell.
Substitute web interactive. Open the Visible Body free preview of blood composition (visiblebody.com — search “blood composition”) or use the freely available Khan Academy module on the components of blood (search “components of the blood Khan Academy”). Either tool lets you rotate or scroll through plasma, red cells, white cells, and platelets at scale.
Time: ~15 minutes in the interactive.
