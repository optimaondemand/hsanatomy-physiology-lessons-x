📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
The heart’s conduction pathway is one of the most cleverly engineered parts of human anatomy: a self-firing oscillator, a deliberate pause, a single insulated cable through the septum, and a network that delivers the signal to every contractile cell within milliseconds. This assignment asks you to show the pathway visually and argue for why the AV-node delay matters.
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Submit your ECG sketch with P, QRS, and T labeled, plus what each represents electrically and mechanically.
2
Submit a photo (or export) of your conduction-tree drawing inside the heart (SA node → Purkinje fibers).
3
Write a 200–250 word response engaging this question: The AV node deliberately slows the electrical signal by about 0.1 seconds. What goes wrong if the delay is too short? What goes wrong if it is too long? Use specific physiological consequences, not just “timing is off.”
4
Identify S1 and S2 in one sentence each: what is making each sound, and at what point in the cycle?
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
ECG sketch correctly labels P, QRS, T with both electrical and mechanical interpretation.
☐
Conduction-tree drawing shows all five conduction components in correct positions.
☐
AV-node delay reasoning specifies what happens too-short (atria + ventricles contract together; atria cannot empty) AND too-long (heart block; bradycardia).
☐
S1 and S2 correctly identified.
