Inside the Heart: Chambers, Valves, and the Septum
Lesson
Module thread — Service. Four valves, never tiring, each opening and closing once per second across an entire human life. Today you make them visible.
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Reading 7.06 — Inside the Heart
Before you start the activities below, read Reading 7.06 end-to-end.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name the three heart wall layers and explain why the left ventricular myocardium is so much thicker than the right.
2
I will be able to name all four valves in the order a drop of blood encounters them, which use chordae tendineae, and trace a full circuit — toe to brain — marking oxygenation status at every structure, valves included.
3
I will be able to describe the difference between valve stenosis and valve insufficiency and give one example of each.

Labeled heart cross-section sketch
20 min
1
Draw a coronal (front-facing) cross-section of the heart in your notebook. Show the four chambers, septum, and all four valves.
2
Draw the left ventricular wall about 3× thicker than the right. Add chordae tendineae and papillary muscles to the AV valves.
3

3
Trace one drop of blood (toe to brain)
15 min
1
Now that you know the valves, finish the trace you started in Lesson 7.05. In your notebook, list every structure a single drop of blood passes through, starting at the toe and ending at the brain. Include all six great vessels, all four chambers, and all four valves, in correct order. (At least 16 named structures.)
2
Mark each structure with O (oxygenated) or D (deoxygenated). Note where the switch happens — there are two switches in a full circuit, not one.
🥽 YOUR VR SESSION THIS WEEK
What to sculpt. Set your sculpted heart from Lesson 7.05 to transparent and open it. Working inside, trace and sculpt:
• The four chambers — the two atria (thin-walled, on top) and the two ventricles (thick-walled, on the bottom; left noticeably thicker than right).
• The interventricular septum — the muscular wall down the middle.
• The four valves: tricuspid (right AV), pulmonary semilunar, mitral (left AV), aortic semilunar. Show the cusps.
• Chordae tendineae and papillary muscles for the two AV valves (small ropes from valve cusps down to muscle bumps on the ventricle floor).
Goal. Make the difference between right ventricle wall (thin) and left ventricle wall (thick) immediately visible. This is one of the most important spatial facts in cardiac anatomy.
Time: ~30 minutes.
