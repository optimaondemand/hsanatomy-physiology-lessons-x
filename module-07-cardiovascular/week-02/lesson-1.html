Not every vessel is visible from straight-on: the pulmonary veins run into the back of the left atrium. Use dashed lines for any vessel (or part of a vessel) that wouldn’t actually be visible from this anterior angle, so your sketch stays anatomically honest.
3
Mark the two “exception” vessels (pulmonary trunk: artery with deoxygenated blood; pulmonary veins: veins with oxygenated blood).
✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK
What to draw. The heart goes on paper today. On a fresh page, draw a large front view of the heart with:
• The pericardial sac, drawn as a dashed outline around the heart so you can see the heart inside.
• The heart itself — the four chambers in their correct spatial arrangement, with the apex down and to the left.
• Both auricles on the upper front of the heart.
• The six great vessels: SVC, IVC, pulmonary trunk (right ventricle), four pulmonary veins (left atrium), aorta (left ventricle).
Color convention. Color the right side and the pulmonary trunk in blue (deoxygenated path). Color the left side, pulmonary veins, and aorta in red (oxygenated path). The vena cavae stay blue. This makes the two circuits visible at a glance.
Time: ~30 minutes.
