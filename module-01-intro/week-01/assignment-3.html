Lesson focus: The Chemistry of Life: Water, pH, and Macromolecules
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
The four biological macromolecule families aren’t only inside your body — they’re listed, by name, on nearly every packaged food in your kitchen. In this assignment you will find a real Nutrition Facts label, identify which macromolecule families it reports, and connect each one to how your body actually uses it. The goal is to practice form-follows-function reasoning on something you can hold in your hand, before you scale it up to tissues, organs, and systems in the modules ahead.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How does the chemistry you just read about show up on the label of an ordinary food in your kitchen?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Go to your kitchen (or pantry) and find one packaged food item with a Nutrition Facts label — a cereal box, a can, a snack wrapper, anything with a label listing carbohydrates, fats, and protein.
2
Take a clear photo of the label and attach it to your submission.
3
List the amounts of carbohydrates, fats (lipids), and protein shown on the label. Name which macromolecule family each one belongs to.
4
For each of the three macros on your label, write 2–3 sentences connecting it to how the body actually uses that family — e.g., the carbohydrates as short-term energy, the fats as long-term energy storage and membrane material, the protein as amino acids for building and repairing tissue.
5
In a final 3–5 sentence paragraph, engage the focus question directly: how does the chemistry from your reading show up on this ordinary label? Do not restate the question — answer it.
6
Total length: 300–400 words, plus your label photo. Submit as a Canvas text entry with the photo attached, or attach a .docx or .pdf containing both.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Attaches a clear, readable photo of a real Nutrition Facts label.
☐
Correctly identifies which macromolecule family each labeled macro belongs to.
☐
Connects each macro to a specific bodily function — not just naming it, but explaining what the body does with it.
☐
Final paragraph engages the focus question with original reasoning.
📊 GRADING RUBRIC
Criteria Excellent (4) Proficient (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1) Pts
Label & identification
4 pts		 Clear label photo attached; all three macros correctly matched to their macromolecule family. Photo attached; one macro misidentified. Photo missing or unreadable, or several misidentifications. No label evidence provided. 4 pts
Macro–function link
4 pts		 All three macros explicitly tied to a specific bodily function. Two of three macros tied to a specific function. Macros named but functions vague or generic. Macros listed with no functional connection. 4 pts
Focus question response
4 pts		 Final paragraph engages the question with clear, original reasoning. Final paragraph addresses the question; reasoning is brief but clear. Final paragraph restates rather than answers. Final paragraph missing or off-topic. 4 pts
