▶ GO DO THIS — PHET SIMULATION
In this lesson you will run an interactive simulation to give pH a feel.
What to do. Open the PhET pH Scale: Basics simulation (free, runs in any browser): phet.colorado.edu/en/simulation/ph-scale-basics. Try the following four substances in order: drain cleaner, milk, blood, lemon juice. For each, predict the pH before you read the meter. Note the H⁺ concentration that appears beneath the pH value — observe how a one-unit pH change is a tenfold change in H⁺.
Goal. Make pH and H⁺ concentration physically meaningful, not just numerical. Notice that the substances inside your body (blood, saliva, gastric acid) span an enormous chemical range.
Time: ~15 minutes.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain why water’s polarity makes it the universal medium for biochemistry.
2
I will be able to describe the pH scale and explain why the body keeps blood pH in such a narrow range.
3
I will be able to name the four families of biological macromolecules, give one example of each from the human body, and link the example to a specific function.
