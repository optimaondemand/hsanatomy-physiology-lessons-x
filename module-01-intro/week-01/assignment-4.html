Lesson focus: Enzymes — Biological Catalysts
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Enzymes work optimally within narrow physiological conditions. Each enzyme in the body is matched to the temperature, pH, and substrate environment of the location where it does its job. In this assignment you will pick one specific enzyme from the body, describe what it does and where, name the conditions it requires, and explain what happens to its activity when those conditions change. The goal is to make “activation energy,” “active site,” “optimum pH,” and “denaturation” concrete — tied to a real reaction in a real body.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How do environmental conditions in the body — temperature, pH, and concentration — control the rates of life’s chemical reactions?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Choose one specific enzyme from the body. Recommended: pepsin, salivary amylase, carbonic anhydrase, or trypsin. (You may choose another specific enzyme you encountered in the reading.)
2
In 2–3 sentences, describe what your enzyme does and where in the body it works.
3
Name and describe the three or four conditions that optimize its activity (temperature, pH, substrate concentration, enzyme concentration). Be specific about the values where possible — e.g., the optimum pH range.
4
Explain what happens to your enzyme’s activity when one of those conditions changes — e.g., what happens to pepsin if it leaves the stomach and enters the small intestine; what happens to salivary amylase in the stomach; what happens to any human enzyme during a 41°C fever.
5
In a final 3–5 sentence paragraph, engage the focus question: how do environmental conditions control the rates of life’s chemistry? Use your enzyme as the central example. Do not restate the question — answer it.
6
Total length: 300–400 words. Submit as Canvas text entry, or attach a .docx or .pdf.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA

Names one specific enzyme and the body location where it works.

📊 GRADING RUBRIC
Criteria Excellent (4) Proficient (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1) Pts
Enzyme accuracy
4 pts		 Enzyme, location, function, and optimal conditions are all accurate. Specific values cited. Mostly accurate; one minor imprecision. Several inaccuracies, or values are vague. Substantial inaccuracies, or no specific enzyme chosen. 4 pts
Conditions and consequence
4 pts		 All four conditions named; clear, specific scenario explaining what happens when one changes. 3–4 conditions named; consequence described, possibly briefly. 2 conditions named; consequence vague. Conditions not named or consequence missing. 4 pts
Focus question response
4 pts		 Final paragraph answers the question with original reasoning grounded in the chosen enzyme. Final paragraph addresses the question; reasoning clear if brief. Final paragraph restates rather than answers. Final paragraph missing or off-topic. 4 pts
