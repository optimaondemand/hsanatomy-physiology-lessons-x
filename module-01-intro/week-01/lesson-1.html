📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Reading 1.01 — What is Anatomy & Physiology? Levels of Organization
The substantive content for this lesson lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before you start the activities below, open Reading 1.01 in Canvas and read it end-to-end. The activities on this page assume you have done so.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to distinguish anatomy from physiology and explain why neither can be fully understood without the other.
2
I will be able to name the six levels of structural organization of the human body, in order, and give one example of each from my own body.
3
I will be able to explain “form follows function” in my own words, using one specific anatomical example.

⚡ TODAY’S ACTIVITIES (80 minutes total)
1
Read Reading 1.01
20 min
1
Open Reading 1.01 in Canvas (the paired reading for this lesson). Read it end-to-end, including the inset on Antonie van Leeuwenhoek.
2
Try the self-check accordion at the bottom of the reading before clicking it. Name the six levels of organization in your head, in order. Then click and verify.
2
Recorded narration
15 min
1
Close Reading 1.01. Without looking back at the text, record yourself speaking (phone, laptop, or Canvas media recorder) a one-paragraph narration (about 150 words) of what you just read — in your own words, out loud, as if explaining it to someone who has not read it.
2
Play your recording back while re-reading the source. What did you remember well? What did you miss? Note one or two corrections in your notebook.
3
Diagram the six levels
15 min
1
On a clean notebook page, draw six horizontal layers, with the smallest level (chemical) at the bottom and the largest (organismal) at the top.
2
At each level, draw and label one example from your own body. (Example: at the cellular level, a red blood cell; at the tissue level, the smooth muscle in your stomach wall; at the organ level, your stomach itself.)
3
Below your diagram, write one sentence explaining what new function emerges as you move up by one level.
✏️ YOUR FIGURE-BUILDING SESSION THIS WEEK
This week’s figure-building is an orientation. You will start one large figure of the human body and add to it across Lessons 1.01 and 1.02; you submit it at the end of the week as your Module 1 Assembled Figure. From Module 3 on, you will build each body system into a figure of your own, a little at a time.
What to do. On your blank page, draw a large outline of a person in anatomical position, front view. Using Reading 1.01 and any anatomy atlas or textbook figure you have, pencil in lightly where you expect the main organs of each of the eleven organ systems to sit, with a short label for each. You will not know all of them yet — that is the point. Put a “?” where you are unsure. Notice which are easy to place and which sit deeper than you expected.
Goal. Build a spatial sense of where things are in the body before any of them are taught in detail. The orientation language you will start using this week — superior, inferior, anterior, posterior, medial, lateral — will become the directional vocabulary for every module after this one.
Time: ~15 minutes.
4
