Lesson focus: What is Anatomy & Physiology? Levels of Organization
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
In Lesson 1.01 you learned that the human body is organized at six hierarchical levels — chemical, cellular, tissue, organ, organ system, organismal — and that each level reveals something different about a body part. In this assignment you will choose one specific body structure and describe it at three different levels of organization, then explain how the level you choose to study changes what you can understand about it. The goal is to practice the kind of thinking you will do every week of this course: looking at the same biological reality from multiple structural depths and pairing every structural observation with a functional consequence.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How does the level of organization at which we choose to study a body part change what we can understand about it?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Re-read the “Six levels of structural organization” section of Reading 1.01, paying attention to how each level builds on the one below it.
2
Choose one body structure to focus on for this assignment. Recommended choices: a single skeletal muscle (e.g., your biceps brachii), the heart, a kidney, or a single neuron. You may pick another structure of similar specificity if you prefer — pick something concrete you can describe at multiple zoom levels.
3
Describe your chosen structure at three different levels of organization, in correct hierarchical order (e.g., cellular → tissue → organ). For each level, write 2–3 sentences naming what you see at that level and what function emerges there.
4
In a final paragraph (3–5 sentences), engage the focus question directly: how does the level you choose to study change what you can understand about your structure? Do not restate the question — answer it.
5
Total length: 300–400 words. Submit as Canvas text entry, or attach a .docx or .pdf.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Names one specific body structure and uses it consistently throughout the response.
☐
Describes the structure clearly at three distinct levels of organization, in correct hierarchical order.
☐
Connects each level to a specific function (form follows function), not merely a description of what is there.
☐
Final paragraph engages the focus question directly — does not restate the prompt.
📊 GRADING RUBRIC
Criteria Excellent (4) Proficient (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1) Pts
Anatomical accuracy
4 pts		 Facts at every level are correct. Anatomical terminology is used precisely. Most facts correct; one minor error or imprecise term. Several factual errors or vague terminology. Substantial inaccuracies or no anatomical terminology. 4 pts
Levels of organization
4 pts		 Three distinct levels named clearly, in correct hierarchical order, with clean separation between them. Three levels named in correct order; some bleed-through between them. Levels are partially distinguished or out of order. Fewer than three levels, or levels not distinguished. 4 pts
Form-follows-function reasoning
4 pts		 Each level’s structure is explicitly tied to a specific function. Final paragraph engages the focus question with original reasoning. Most levels tied to a function. Final paragraph addresses the question. Function described at one level only, or final paragraph restates rather than answers. Description-only response with no functional reasoning. 4 pts
Total: 12 pts
