📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 5.06 — Cerebrum: Cortex, Lobes, Ventricles, Meninges
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 5.06 and complete Lesson 5.06, including the brain refinement drawing.
Lesson focus: The cerebral cortex is organized contralaterally and by lobe; the CNS is protected by ventricles full of CSF and three meningeal layers.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three parts: a labeled lateral-view cortex diagram, a labeled meninges cross-section, and a short clinical-reasoning paragraph using contralateral organization.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why does damage to one side of the brain produce symptoms on the opposite side of the body?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Labeled cortex diagram. Submit your lateral-view brain sketch. Required labels: the four lobes, central sulcus, lateral sulcus, primary motor cortex, primary somatosensory cortex, primary visual cortex, Broca’s area, Wernicke’s area.
2
Meninges cross-section. Submit your meninges cross-section sketch with five labels: skull bone, dura mater, arachnoid mater, subarachnoid space (with CSF), pia mater. Show the cortex underneath.
3
Clinical-reasoning paragraph (5–7 sentences). A 58-year-old patient comes to the ER. She can no longer move her right arm or right leg, and she has trouble producing speech (though she understands it). Predict (a) which hemisphere of her brain has been damaged, (b) which lobe(s) and which named cortex areas, and (c) why the symptoms are on the right side of her body. Engage the focus question.
4
Total length: two diagrams + 150–225 words of writing. Submit as Canvas text entry with image attachments, or as .docx / .pdf with images embedded.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All nine cortex labels correct and correctly placed.
☐
All five meninges layers labeled in correct order.
☐
Clinical reasoning correctly identifies left hemisphere; frontal lobe; primary motor cortex (for the weakness) and Broca’s area (for the speech production loss); explains pyramidal decussation.
