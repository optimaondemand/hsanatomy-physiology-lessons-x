📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 5.07 — The Spinal Cord
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 5.07 and complete Lesson 5.07, including the VR cross-section sculpt.
Lesson focus: A spinal cord cross-section shows the anatomical separation of sensory in and motor out, and explains why injury level determines what is lost.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has two parts: a fully labeled spinal cord cross-section, and a short clinical-reasoning paragraph about a hypothetical spinal cord injury.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why does the spinal cord have the specific cross-sectional anatomy it does — sensory in at one end, motor out at the other?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Labeled cross-section. Submit either your hand-drawn cross-section or a screenshot of the VR sculpt. Required labels: gray matter, white matter, dorsal horn, ventral horn, lateral horn, central canal, dorsal root, dorsal root ganglion, ventral root, spinal nerve. Arrows must show direction of signal flow (sensory in via dorsal root; motor out via ventral root).
2
Spinal injury reasoning (6–9 sentences). A 22-year-old man dives into a shallow lake and injures his spinal cord at the C4–C5 level. Describe (a) what motor function he will lose, (b) what sensory function he will lose, (c) whether he can breathe on his own, and (d) one named ascending or descending tract you can be confident is now disrupted at that level. Engage the focus question.
3
Total length: diagram + 175–275 words of writing. Submit as Canvas text entry with image attachment, or as .docx / .pdf with image embedded.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All ten cross-section labels correctly placed; signal-flow arrows correct.
☐
Reasoning correctly predicts tetraplegia, sensory loss below the lesion, diaphragm uncertainty (C3–C5 is borderline — partial sparing possible), names one specific tract.
☐
Focus question engaged with reasoning about why separating sensory and motor anatomically is functionally efficient.
