Module thread — Courage. The cerebrum decides. The cerebellum smooths the deciding. The brainstem keeps you alive while the deciding happens. All three need to be working for you to have the courage to act.

✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK
This week is your first brain drawing session. You will draw the four major brain divisions on a large side view of the brain, color-coding them so the boundaries are visible at a glance. This brain page is one of the detail pages required in your end-of-module Assembled Figure.
What to do. On a fresh page (paper highly recommended), draw a large side view of the head with the brain inside the skull outline, using the figures in Reading 5.05 as your reference. Then color in the four divisions:
▶ Brainstem — color #1 (e.g., red). Highlight the medulla, pons, and midbrain from bottom to top. Label all three subregions.
▶ Cerebellum — color #2 (e.g., green). Draw the “little brain” behind and below the cerebrum, against the back of the brainstem.
▶ Diencephalon — color #3 (e.g., yellow). This is hidden inside; draw it with a dashed outline, as if looking through the cerebrum. Highlight the thalamus, hypothalamus, and (if your drawing scale allows it) the pineal gland of the epithalamus separately.
▶ Cerebrum — color #4 (e.g., blue). The largest, outermost mass. Note that the cerebrum covers most of the diencephalon from above.
Step back and check your drawing from the side once coloring is complete: cerebrum on top, cerebellum at the back, brainstem extending downward into the spinal cord, diencephalon hidden in the middle.
