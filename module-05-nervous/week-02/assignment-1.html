📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 5.05 — The Brain: Four Major Divisions
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 5.05 and complete the activities in Lesson 5.05, including the VR brain divisions tracing.
Lesson focus: Each major division of the brain has a distinct location, distinct anatomy, and distinct functions — damage to each looks different.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment combines a labeled brain sketch with a clinical-style reasoning exercise. Given four short patient descriptions, you decide which brain division is most likely damaged in each case.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If you observe a patient’s symptoms, can you predict which part of the brain has been injured? Why does this work?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Labeled brain sketch. Submit a photograph of your lateral-view brain sketch from the lesson. All four divisions must be labeled and color-coded. The three subregions of the brainstem (medulla, pons, midbrain) must also be labeled.
2
Case-matching task. Read the four short patient descriptions below. For each, identify which brain division is most likely the site of damage and give a one-sentence justification.

Case A: A 67-year-old man has had a stroke. He is alive and breathing on his own, but unable to walk without staggering, and his finger-to-nose test is wildly inaccurate — he overshoots the target every time.

Case B: A 45-year-old woman is brought to the ER after a severe head injury. She is unconscious but breathing. Hours later, she stops breathing and her heart rate becomes erratic. She does not survive.

Case C: A 72-year-old man had a small stroke six months ago. He has lost the ability to produce fluent speech — he understands questions and seems mentally sharp, but his speech is halting, effortful, and ungrammatical.

Case D: A 28-year-old woman has a benign tumor pressing on a deep structure in her brain. Her body temperature has become unstable, she has uncontrolled thirst, and her sleep cycles are disrupted.
3
Focus-question paragraph (3–5 sentences). Engage the focus question. Explain in your own words why specific brain damage produces specific, predictable symptoms — the principle of functional localization that Broca established.
4
Total length: sketch + 4 case matches with justifications + 75–125 word paragraph. Submit as Canvas text entry with image attachment, or as .docx / .pdf with image embedded.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Brain sketch labels all four divisions and the three brainstem subregions correctly.
☐
All four cases correctly matched (A = cerebellum, B = brainstem, C = cerebrum / Broca’s area, D = diencephalon / hypothalamus); each justification names the specific feature that points to the division.
☐
Focus paragraph names functional localization explicitly or describes the idea correctly.
