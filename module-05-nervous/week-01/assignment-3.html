📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 5.03 — The Synapse
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 5.03 and complete the activities in Lesson 5.03, including the VR synapse sculpt.
Lesson focus: A chemical synapse converts an electrical signal in one cell into a chemical signal in the cleft and back into an electrical signal in the next cell.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has two parts: a labeled synapse diagram, and a written paragraph that traces a signal from action potential to the next cell’s response. Both are drawn from Reading 5.03 and your VR synapse sculpt.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
A signal between two neurons is electrical, then chemical, then electrical again. Why does the body bother with the chemical step in the middle?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Labeled synapse diagram. Submit a photograph of your hand-drawn synapse or a screenshot of your VR synapse sculpt. Your diagram must label all six parts: presynaptic terminal, synaptic vesicles, synaptic cleft, neurotransmitter, postsynaptic membrane, neurotransmitter receptors.
2
Five-step signal trace (5–8 sentences). Trace the signal from the arrival of an action potential at the presynaptic terminal to the change in voltage on the postsynaptic membrane. Name calcium’s role and vesicles’ role. Identify the step where the signal is cleared from the cleft.
3
Focus-question paragraph (3–5 sentences). Engage the focus question directly. Why a chemical middle step? Name at least one benefit it gives the nervous system (one-way flow, signal amplification, room for modulation, room for diverse neurotransmitter effects, etc.). Do not restate the question.
4
Total length: diagram + 175–250 words of writing. Submit as Canvas text entry with image attachment, or as .docx / .pdf with the image embedded.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All six synapse parts labeled and correctly placed.
☐
Five-step signal trace correct and in order; calcium and vesicles named.
☐
Focus-question paragraph names at least one specific advantage of the chemical step.
☐
Terminology used correctly: presynaptic, postsynaptic, vesicle, cleft, neurotransmitter, receptor.
