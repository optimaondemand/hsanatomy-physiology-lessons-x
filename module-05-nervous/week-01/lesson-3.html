Module thread — Courage. Most of what happens in your nervous system is not transmission — it is decision. At every synapse, a vote is cast and a sum is taken. The brain has the courage to act because thousands of these sums settle in its favor every second.

✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK
This is your second drawing session of Module 5. You will draw one chemical synapse at exaggerated scale on its own detail page. This synapse is one of the detail pages required in your end-of-module Assembled Figure.
What to do. Take out the neuron you drew in Lesson 5.02. On a fresh page, draw a close-up of one of its axon terminals, then build the postsynaptic side facing it.
▶ Draw one axon terminal, enlarged into a clear bulb shape. Label it presynaptic terminal.
▶ Inside the bulb, draw 6–10 small spheres representing synaptic vesicles, each one packed with neurotransmitter. Cluster a few of them touching the bottom inner edge of the bulb (the “ready to release” pool).
▶ Below the presynaptic terminal, leave a small gap (the synaptic cleft) and draw a flat or gently curved postsynaptic membrane representing the dendrite of the next neuron.
▶ On the postsynaptic membrane, draw 4–6 small bumps representing neurotransmitter receptors.
▶ In the cleft, scatter a few tiny dots representing free neurotransmitter molecules that have been released and are crossing to the receptors.
Label every part: presynaptic terminal, synaptic vesicles, synaptic cleft, neurotransmitter, postsynaptic membrane, neurotransmitter receptors. Add an arrow showing the direction the signal travels (presynaptic → postsynaptic).
Keep this page. This synapse is one of the required detail pages for your module-end Assembled Figure.
