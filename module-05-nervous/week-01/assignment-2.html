📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 5.02 — The Neuron at Work
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 5.02 and complete the activities in Lesson 5.02, including the neuron drawing.
Lesson focus: Every signal in the body is a controlled discharge of a battery the neuron actively maintains.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has two parts: an annotated action-potential graph, and a written paragraph that explains why an action potential is “all-or-nothing” and only travels in one direction. Both are drawn from Reading 5.02 and the action-potential graph activity in your lesson notebook.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If an action potential is always the same size no matter how strong the stimulus, how does the nervous system encode the difference between a light touch and a firm one?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Annotated action-potential graph. Submit a photograph or scan of your hand-drawn action-potential curve from the lesson activity. Your graph must label: resting potential (−70 mV), threshold (−55 mV), peak (+30 mV), and after-hyperpolarization. Annotate three phases — depolarization, repolarization, hyperpolarization — with the ion movement (Na⁺ in / K⁺ out) and the channel state.
2
“All-or-nothing” paragraph (6–8 sentences). Answer the focus question. Explain what “all-or-nothing” means at the level of a single action potential. Then explain how the nervous system actually encodes stimulus intensity (more neurons firing, higher firing rate). Use the word threshold.
3
One-sentence one-direction note. Write one sentence explaining why an action potential travels only one way down the axon, using the term refractory period.
4
Total length: graph + 150–225 words of writing. Submit as Canvas text entry plus image attachment, or as a single .docx / .pdf with the graph embedded.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All four voltage landmarks labeled on the graph; three phases annotated with ion movement and channel state.
☐
Paragraph correctly defines “all-or-nothing” and identifies at least one mechanism (firing rate or neuron recruitment) by which intensity is encoded.
☐
Refractory-period sentence is correct (the patch behind the wave cannot fire again, so the signal moves forward).
☐
Anatomical and physiological terminology used precisely: threshold, sodium, potassium, depolarization, repolarization, hyperpolarization, refractory period.
