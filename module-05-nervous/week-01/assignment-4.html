📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 5.04 — Reflex Arcs
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 5.04 and complete the lesson activities, including the VR reflex tracing.
Lesson focus: Reflex arcs are the simplest functional circuits in the nervous system; every one has the same five components.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment asks you to diagram a reflex arc twice — once at the anatomical level (the path traced on the body figure in VR) and once at the circuit level (the five components flat in your notebook) — and to explain what would go wrong if the brain were inserted into the loop.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why is it dangerous, evolutionarily, to require conscious thought before pulling your hand off a hot stove?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Circuit-level diagram. Submit a photograph of your hand-drawn withdrawal-reflex circuit. All five components must be labeled (sensory receptor, sensory neuron, integration center / interneuron, motor neuron, effector). At least one inhibitory branch to the antagonist (triceps) must be shown.
2
Anatomical-level diagram. Submit a screenshot of your VR on-body reflex tracing, with the five components labeled along the actual anatomy (fingertip → spinal cord at C6–C8 → biceps brachii).
3
Focus-question paragraph (5–7 sentences). Answer the focus question. Use specific time numbers (reflex ~200 ms, conscious sensation ~300–400 ms) and at least one specific consequence of routing the signal through the cortex first.
4
Total length: two diagrams + 175–275 words of writing. Submit as Canvas text entry with image attachments, or as .docx / .pdf with images embedded.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All five components labeled on the circuit-level diagram, plus inhibitory branch to antagonist.
☐
VR tracing shows the same five components on the actual body anatomy, with the spinal-cord integration at C6–C8.
☐
Focus-question paragraph uses concrete numbers and names a specific cost of routing through the brain.
☐
Terminology used correctly: afferent, efferent, interneuron, monosynaptic, polysynaptic.
