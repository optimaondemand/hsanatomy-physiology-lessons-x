🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to distinguish the central nervous system (CNS) from the peripheral nervous system (PNS) by location and function.
2
I will be able to describe the difference between somatic and autonomic divisions of the PNS, with one example of each in everyday life.
3
I will be able to label the four parts of a neuron (dendrites, cell body, axon, axon terminals) and state which direction a signal travels through them.
4
I will be able to name four glial cell types and one job each one does.
5
I will be able to describe what Ramón y Cajal proposed in the “neuron doctrine” and why it was contested at the time.

✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK
This week is a high-level orientation — you will not yet be drawing individual neurons. The goal is to see the entire nervous system as one continuous structure laid over a body you already know. You will start your Nervous System Map, a body outline that you build up across the module.
What to do. On a large page (paper highly recommended; a digital drawing file works too), draw an outline of a person in anatomical position, front view, and title it “Nervous System Map.” Keep your Module 4 Muscle Map beside you as a guide. Then, in this order:
▶ The CNS. Draw the brain inside the cranium, and the spinal cord running down the midline (draw it as a dashed line, since it lies behind the front surface). These two structures together are the entire central nervous system. Add a label on each.
▶ The PNS. Now draw the nerves running out from the brain (cranial nerves) and from the spinal cord (spinal nerves): a sprawling web extending into every region of the body. Draw at least one cranial nerve and one spinal nerve from origin to a peripheral destination.
▶ The connection to muscle. Pick one major skeletal muscle from your Module 4 work (the biceps brachii is easy) and draw it on your outline. Draw a peripheral nerve from that muscle back to the spinal cord. Notice the path. This is the literal pathway every command for that muscle travels.
End with a 60-second tour. Point to each region of your map (head, neck, torso, limb) and name out loud whether you are looking at CNS or PNS.
