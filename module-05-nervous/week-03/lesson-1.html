The Autonomic Nervous System — Fight or Flight, Rest or Digest

Module thread — Courage. The nervous system you never see is what stands between you and panic. Sympathetic mobilizes; parasympathetic restores. Real courage is built on both.

1
I will be able to distinguish sympathetic from parasympathetic by outflow location (thoracolumbar vs craniosacral) and by primary neurotransmitter (NE vs ACh).
2

3

I will be able to describe Walter Cannon’s contribution to physiology (“fight or flight” and homeostasis).

✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK
This session adds the autonomic wiring to your Nervous System Map. You will draw the sympathetic chain on one side of the spinal column, and the parasympathetic outflow (especially the vagus nerve) coming down from the brainstem.
▶ Sympathetic chain. On your Nervous System Map, draw the small string of paired ganglia that run alongside the spinal column, from upper thoracic down to upper lumbar levels, on one side (left or right). Mark at least three ganglia and use a color (e.g., red) to indicate sympathetic fibers. Add a label noting the “T1–L2 thoracolumbar outflow.”
▶ Vagus nerve (parasympathetic). Find the vagus (CN X) on your Week 2 cranial-nerve page. Now draw it on your map from the brainstem all the way down: through the neck, into the thorax, branching to the heart and lungs, and continuing into the abdomen to the stomach, liver, and most of the gut. Use a second color (e.g., blue). This single nerve carries roughly 75% of the parasympathetic output of the entire body.
▶ Adrenal medulla note. Draw the adrenal glands sitting on top of the kidneys. Add a small marker on the adrenal medulla — note that it is functionally a sympathetic ganglion that releases epinephrine into the blood. You will revisit this in Module 6.
Keep your map. The autonomic wiring is part of your module-end Assembled Figure.
