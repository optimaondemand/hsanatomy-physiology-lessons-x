📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 5.10 — The Eye and Vision
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 5.10 and complete Lesson 5.10, including the eye drawing.
Lesson focus: The eye is built like a camera; every part exists to get focused light onto the retina and turn it into nerve signal.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has two parts: a labeled eye cross-section, and a numbered trace of the light-then-signal path from cornea to visual cortex.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Every part of the eye exists for a reason — pick one structure and explain how its form follows its function.
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Labeled eye diagram. Submit a photograph (or digital export) of your eye drawing. Required labels: cornea, sclera, iris, pupil, ciliary body, lens, choroid, retina, fovea, optic disc, optic nerve, aqueous humor, vitreous humor.
2
Light-and-signal trace (numbered). Write a numbered sequence starting with light from the world and ending in the primary visual cortex. Name every structure the light or signal passes through: cornea → aqueous humor → pupil → lens → vitreous humor → retina (rods/cones) → bipolar cells → ganglion cells → optic nerve (CN II) → optic chiasm → lateral geniculate nucleus of thalamus → primary visual cortex of the occipital lobe. At least 10 steps.
3
Form-follows-function paragraph (3–5 sentences). Engage the focus question. Pick one structure (lens, iris, fovea, or another) and explain how its specific shape or composition fits its specific job.
4
Total length: diagram + numbered trace + 75–150 word paragraph. Submit as Canvas text entry with image attachment, or as .docx / .pdf with image embedded.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All 13 eye labels correctly placed.
☐
Light-and-signal trace has at least 10 ordered steps with structures named correctly.
☐
Form-follows-function paragraph names a specific structure and a specific shape-to-function connection.
