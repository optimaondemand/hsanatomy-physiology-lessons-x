📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Reading 5.12 — Touch, the Somatosensory System, and Brain-Body Integration
The substantive content for this lesson lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before you start the activities below, open Reading 5.12 and read it end-to-end.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name the five submodalities of somatosensation and one kind of receptor for each.
2
I will be able to trace a touch signal from skin receptor to primary somatosensory cortex, naming every structure in between.
3
I will be able to explain why the somatosensory homunculus is disproportionate — and connect that to the receptor density of different body regions.
4
I will be able to describe what a complete nervous system looks like — brain, cord, PNS, autonomic wiring, sense organs — and assemble all of it from memory.

✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK — REASSEMBLY
This is the module-end reassembly session. You will combine everything you have drawn in Weeks 1, 2, and 3 into a single complete nervous-system figure. The photograph or export from this session is the deliverable for the Module 5 Assembled Figure Assignment.
What to do. Gather your Nervous System Map and every detail page from Weeks 1–3, and lay them out together on a table or the floor (or on one slide, if you are working digitally). Verify each of the following is present and labeled:
▶ Brain — four major divisions (brainstem, cerebellum, diencephalon, cerebrum) and four cerebral lobes (frontal, parietal, temporal, occipital) shown.
▶ Ventricular system shown with dashed outlines, with all four ventricles marked.
▶ Meninges — three layers wrapping the brain (dura, arachnoid, pia).
▶ Spinal cord running through the vertebral column, with at least one cross-section detailed.
▶ All 12 cranial nerves labeled at brainstem origins.
▶ Spinal nerves shown at cervical, thoracic, lumbar, and sacral levels (representative nerves; do not need every pair).
▶ Autonomic wiring — sympathetic chain on one side; vagus nerve from brainstem to abdomen.
▶ Eye with cornea, lens, retina, optic nerve.
▶ Ear with outer, middle, inner regions and CN VIII.
Detail pages to include alongside the map: one synapse (with vesicles and receptors); the spinal cord cross-section. Both should be visible in the same photograph, beside the body map.
Photograph or export your figure when it is at its best. Compose the shot so the brain, the spinal cord, and the major nerve pathways are visible. Save the photo with a descriptive name and prepare it for upload to the Module 5 Assembled Figure Assignment.
