📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 5.09 — The Autonomic Nervous System
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 5.09 and complete Lesson 5.09, including the sympathetic + vagus drawing.
Lesson focus: Sympathetic and parasympathetic act as opposing branches; almost every organ has dual innervation with continuous tonic balance.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has two parts: a completed sympathetic-vs-parasympathetic comparison table and three scenarios with predicted physiological responses, plus a short paragraph on dual innervation.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why does the body need two opposing autonomic branches instead of just one with an on/off switch?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Comparison table. Submit your hand-written or scanned eight-row sympathetic vs parasympathetic comparison from the lesson. (Nickname, outflow, target neurotransmitter, heart rate, pupils, airways, digestion, sweating.)
2
Scenario predictions (table form). For each of three scenarios, predict the dominant branch and the effect on four target systems: heart rate, pupils, airways, gut motility.

Scenario A: You step into a crosswalk and a car swerves toward you.
Scenario B: You are reading a book under a blanket after a meal.
Scenario C: You are catching your breath after sprinting up a flight of stairs.
3
Dual-innervation paragraph (4–6 sentences). Engage the focus question. Explain in your own words why dual innervation with continuous tonic balance is better than a single on/off system. Use the word tone at least once.
4
Total length: comparison table + 3 scenario predictions + 100–175 word paragraph. Submit as Canvas text entry with image attachment, or as .docx / .pdf with images embedded.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Comparison table has all eight rows filled correctly for both branches.
☐
All three scenarios show correct dominant branch and correct directional predictions for HR, pupils, airways, gut.
☐
Paragraph engages the focus question and uses “tone” correctly.
