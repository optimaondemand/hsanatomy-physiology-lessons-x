Lesson focus: The Integumentary System — Skin as the First Organ System
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Skin is the cleanest example in the body of structure-tied-to-function. In this assignment you will pick one specific function skin performs (thermoregulation, barrier protection, vitamin D synthesis, sensation, or immune defense) and trace it through the structures of the integument — layers and accessory structures alike. The goal is the same skill you have practiced all module — pairing form to function — now applied to a complete organ system.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How does the layered, multi-tissue structure of skin make it possible for one organ to do so many different jobs at once?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Choose one function of skin to focus on: thermoregulation, barrier protection, vitamin D synthesis, sensation, or immune defense.
2
In a paragraph (4–6 sentences), describe the function and why it matters for the body.
3
In a longer paragraph (5–7 sentences), name at least three specific structures in the integument that contribute to your chosen function — at least one from a layer (epidermis, dermis, hypodermis) and at least one accessory structure (hair follicle, nail, sweat gland, sebaceous gland). For each, explain how its structure (cell type, tissue, location) enables its part of the function. Be specific. (Example for thermoregulation: eccrine sweat glands in the dermis produce a watery secretion that pulls heat from the body as it evaporates…)
4
In a final 3–5 sentence paragraph, engage the focus question. How does skin’s layered, multi-tissue architecture allow one organ to do so many different jobs at once? Use your chosen function as the central example. Do not restate the question.
5
Total length: 350–450 words. Submit as Canvas text entry, or attach a .docx or .pdf.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
