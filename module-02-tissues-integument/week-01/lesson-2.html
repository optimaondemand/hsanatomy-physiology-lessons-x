✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK
Today you draw representative cells, not an organ. The point is to make muscle fibers and neurons feel like real, three-dimensional objects — not just diagrams — by drawing them yourself, at exaggerated scale, and thinking about how their shapes serve their jobs.
What to do. On a clean page (paper highly recommended), draw two representative cells at exaggerated scale. Draw first from memory of Reading 2.02, then check the reading’s figures and correct your drawing in a second color. (1) A skeletal muscle fiber. A long thin cylinder. Mark several nuclei along its length (skeletal muscle is multi-nucleated). Show striations as repeating bands across the length of the fiber. (2) A neuron. A roughly spherical cell body with a single nucleus. Multiple short branched dendrites coming off the cell body. One long axon extending away. (Optional: wrap segments of the axon in “myelin” pieces, with small gaps between — the nodes where the action potential will jump.)
Goal. Build mental models of the cells you will return to in Modules 4, 5, and 7. The shapes you draw today are the shapes you will encounter in detail later.
Time: ~15 minutes.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to distinguish skeletal, cardiac, and smooth muscle by structure, location, and voluntary vs. involuntary control.
2
I will be able to name the three structural parts of a neuron and explain what each does.
3
I will be able to describe, in general terms, why excitability depends on ions moving across the cell membrane — without yet needing the full electrical detail that comes in Module 5.
