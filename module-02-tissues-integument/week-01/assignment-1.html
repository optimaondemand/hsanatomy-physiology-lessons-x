Lesson focus: Epithelial and Connective Tissues
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Each tissue in the body is shaped to do a specific job. Simple squamous epithelium is one cell thick because thin layers diffuse fast. Tendons run their collagen fibers in one direction because that is the direction they need to resist pull. In this assignment you will pick three specific body locations and explain why the tissue at each is built the way it is.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How does the structure of a tissue at a given body location predict the function it must perform there?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Choose three body locations from the list below, or pick three of your own at similar specificity:
  • The lining of a blood capillary
  • The outer surface of the skin
  • The lining of the small intestine
  • The Achilles tendon
  • The cartilage at the end of the femur
  • The fat layer beneath the skin (hypodermis)
2
For each location, do three things in 3–4 sentences each: (a) name the tissue type and the specific subtype (e.g., “simple squamous epithelium” or “dense regular connective tissue”), (b) describe its structural features (cell shape, layers, fiber arrangement, matrix), (c) explain how those features match the function the tissue performs at that location.
3
In a final 3–5 sentence paragraph, engage the focus question: how does structure predict function for tissues? Use observations from your three locations as evidence. Do not restate the question.
4
Total length: 350–450 words. Submit as Canvas text entry, or attach a .docx or .pdf.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Names three specific body locations and the correct tissue subtype at each.
☐
Describes the structural features of each tissue accurately and specifically.
☐
Connects each tissue’s structure to its specific function at that body location.
☐
Final paragraph engages the focus question with evidence from the three examples.
