✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK
Today you begin drawing the cross-section of the integument (skin) that you will build up through Lesson 2.04 and submit at the end of the week as your Module 2 Assembled Figure. The point of starting here is to anchor today’s tissue content to a real organ that uses both epithelial and connective tissue together.
What to do. On a large page (paper highly recommended; a digital drawing file is also fine), sketch a simplified vertical cross-section of skin. Make it tall and wide — you will add to it all week, so leave room. Draw three horizontal layers stacked on top of each other and label them: epidermis (the top layer, stratified squamous epithelium), dermis (middle, dense irregular connective tissue), hypodermis (bottom, loose / adipose connective tissue). Don’t worry about detail today — the layers and labels are enough. You will add accessory structures (hair follicle, sweat gland, sebaceous gland) in Lesson 2.04.
Goal. Make the four tissue types concrete. Skin is the body’s simplest organ to draw because two of the four tissue types do almost all the work in it.
Time: ~15 minutes.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name the three structural features that define epithelial tissue and explain why each one matters.
2
I will be able to classify any epithelium by number of layers and cell shape — including pseudostratified columnar and transitional epithelium — and give one body location for each major type.
3
I will be able to distinguish loose, dense, and specialized connective tissues, and name an example of each.
