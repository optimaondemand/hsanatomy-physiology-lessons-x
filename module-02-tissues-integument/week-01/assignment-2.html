Lesson focus: Muscle and Nervous Tissues
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Muscle tissue contracts. Nervous tissue signals. Different jobs, different cells — but they share one trait: excitability. Both maintain a voltage across their cell membranes, and both can rapidly change that voltage to do their work. In this assignment you will compare a muscle cell and a neuron, identifying what makes each one specialized and what they have in common. Keep your explanation of excitability at the level of today’s reading and animation — ion movement through membrane channels — rather than inventing electrical detail you have not yet studied; the full picture arrives in Module 5.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
What makes muscle and nervous tissue distinct from epithelial and connective tissue — and what makes a muscle cell distinct from a neuron?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Choose one muscle type (skeletal, cardiac, or smooth) to focus on, alongside the neuron.
2
Describe your chosen muscle cell in 4–5 sentences: shape, size, striations or not, number of nuclei, voluntary or involuntary, and one body location where it works.
3
Describe a neuron in 4–5 sentences: name and describe the cell body, dendrites, and axon. Mention myelin and what it does.
4
In 3–4 sentences, explain what excitability is, and how both your muscle cell and the neuron use it. Use the words action potential and ion channels, and describe (in plain terms, as in today’s activity) how ions moving across the membrane creates the signal.
5
In a final 3–5 sentence paragraph, engage the focus question. What makes muscle and nervous tissue different from epithelial and connective tissue, and what makes your muscle cell different from a neuron despite their shared excitability? Do not restate the question.
6
Total length: 350–450 words. Submit as Canvas text entry, or attach a .docx or .pdf.
