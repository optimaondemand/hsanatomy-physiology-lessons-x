📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 4.10 — Muscle Metabolism: Energy, Fatigue, and Fiber Types
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 4.10 and complete the activities in Lesson 4.10.
Lesson focus: Three ATP systems power the cross-bridge cycle. Different fibers favor different systems.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment is physiology, not anatomy. (1) Compare the three ATP systems. (2) Apply the systems to two specific real-world activities. (3) Connect fiber types to athletic specialization. (4) Contrast ordinary, reversible muscle fatigue with a real mitochondrial disease that fatigue does not fix.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why does a champion marathon runner have a different muscle-fiber profile from a champion sprinter, and what role does training play vs. genetics?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Three energy systems table. Three rows: creatine phosphate, anaerobic glycolysis, aerobic respiration. Columns: name, duration it sustains, oxygen required (Y/N), ATP per glucose (where applicable), one example activity.
2
Two-activity application. Pick two activities: (a) a single max-effort dead lift, and (b) a 5-kilometer run at moderate pace. For each, state which energy system(s) dominate and roughly for how long. Explain in 2–3 sentences why each activity recruits the systems it does. (~150 words total.)
3
Focus-question answer (4–6 sentences). Compare the fiber profile of a marathon runner (high Type I) with a sprinter (high Type IIx). Reference (a) the speed difference, (b) the fatigue resistance difference, and (c) the role of genetics vs. training. Be honest about what training can and cannot change.
4
Fatigue vs. mitochondrial disease (4–5 sentences). First, name one contributor to ordinary muscle fatigue from the reading (ion imbalance, glycogen depletion, lactate / pH change, or Pi buildup) and explain why rest reverses it — a fatigued muscle is not a damaged one. Then contrast this with mitochondrial myopathy: a group of genetic diseases in which a muscle fiber’s mitochondria cannot keep up with ATP demand, even though the sarcomeres and the NMJ work normally. Using what you know about the three fiber types, predict whether slow oxidative (Type I) or fast glycolytic (Type IIx) fibers would be affected earlier and more severely, and explain why rest does not restore function the way it does in ordinary fatigue.
5
Total length: table + 350–500 words.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA

☐

☐
