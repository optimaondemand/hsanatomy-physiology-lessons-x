📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 4.09 — Muscles of the Pelvis and Lower Limb
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 4.09 and complete the activities in Lesson 4.09.
Lesson focus: Antagonist pairs at the hip, knee, and ankle let you walk, run, and stand.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Lower-limb atlas + a gastrocnemius-vs-soleus question that depends on knowing which joints each muscle crosses.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why is the soleus the muscle most responsible for keeping you upright while standing, but the gastrocnemius is the muscle most responsible for the explosive push-off when sprinting?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
OIA table. Seven rows: gluteus maximus, iliopsoas, quadriceps femoris (note the four heads), hamstrings (note the three named muscles), tibialis anterior, gastrocnemius, soleus.
2
Labeled Muscle Map photo. Front and back of figure showing all seven muscles / groups in their colors. The four quadriceps heads should be distinguishable.
3
Focus-question answer (4–6 sentences). Answer the focus question. Address (a) the difference in origins (gastrocnemius from the femur, soleus from the tibia / fibula), (b) which joints each muscle crosses, and (c) why the gastrocnemius’s ability to cross the knee makes it more powerful for sprinting push-off.
4
Total length: OIA table + Muscle Map photo + 150–250 words.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All seven OIA rows accurate; all four quadriceps heads and all three hamstring muscles named.
☐
Muscle Map photo shows all muscles in clearly distinguishable colors.
☐
Focus-question answer correctly distinguishes the two calf muscles by which joints they cross.
