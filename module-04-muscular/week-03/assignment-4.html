📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 4.12 — Homeostatic Imbalances of the Muscular System
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 4.12 and complete the activities in Lesson 4.12, including the final figure.
Lesson focus: Every named condition in this reading fails at a specific, identifiable point in the contraction chain.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This is the module’s culminating project. You will choreograph or freestyle a short video — “Muscles in Motion” — set to a song of your choice, made of 25 distinct moves. For each move, you will name the muscle(s) primarily responsible. For example: blowing a kiss to the camera uses the orbicularis oris (puckering the lips), and the biceps brachii and triceps brachii (raising and moving the arm). This is the same identification skill every atlas assignment this module has been building toward, applied to your own body in motion.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If you had myasthenia gravis, which of your 25 moves would tire out first — and why?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
The video. Film yourself performing 25 distinct moves to a song of your choice (1–3 minutes total). Moves can be dance steps, gestures, poses, or a mix — the requirement is 25 visibly different actions, not 25 seconds of the same motion repeated. Keep it appropriate for a school submission.
2
The muscle list. A numbered list of all 25 moves, each with 1–3 muscles (by name, using this module’s vocabulary) primarily responsible for that move. At least 15 different named muscles / groups must appear somewhere across the list — you should be drawing on all three weeks, not just one region.
3
Focus-question answer (3–5 sentences). Answer the focus question. Name the move, name the muscle(s) it depends on, and explain — using what Reading 4.12 taught you about myasthenia gravis — why a muscle used repeatedly across many moves would be the first to fail.
4
Total submission: video + numbered muscle list + focus-question answer.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Video shows 25 genuinely distinct moves.
☐
Muscle list is complete (25 entries) and names muscles accurately for each move; at least 15 distinct muscles / groups appear across the list.
☐
Focus-question answer correctly connects repeated-use fatigue to the mechanism of myasthenia gravis.
