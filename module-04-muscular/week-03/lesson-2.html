✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK — REASSEMBLY
This is the major reassembly session of Module 4. You will combine every muscle you have drawn over the past two weeks — head and neck, back, thorax, abdomen, upper limb, lower limb — into one complete Muscle Map. The assembled Muscle Map is the basis of your module Assembled Figure in Lesson 4.12.
What to do. Gather your Muscle Map pages from Lessons 4.01 and 4.05 through 4.09 (front and back views plus the head-and-neck close-up). Check each against its lesson, one at a time, in this order, adding anything missing or unlabeled:
▶ Head and neck (8 muscles, from Lesson 4.05).
▶ Back and thorax (trapezius, latissimus, erector spinae, intercostals, diaphragm — from Lesson 4.06).
▶ Abdominal wall (rectus abdominis + 3 obliques, from Lesson 4.07).
▶ Upper limb (deltoid, biceps, triceps, forearm flexors, forearm extensors — from Lesson 4.08).
▶ Lower limb (gluteus maximus, iliopsoas, quadriceps, hamstrings, tibialis anterior, gastrocnemius, soleus — from Lesson 4.09).
▶ Detail pages — place your muscle cross-section (Lesson 4.02), your sarcomere and its cycle frames (Lesson 4.03), and your NMJ (Lesson 4.04) beside the Muscle Map.
Once everything is gathered, lay the pages out together on a table or the floor (or on one slide, if you are working digitally), with the front and back views side by side and the detail pages beside them. Re-label anything faint or missing. Photograph a preliminary version. You will refine it for submission in Lesson 4.12. Keep every page together.
Time: ~40 minutes.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name the three ATP-producing systems of a working muscle (creatine phosphate, anaerobic glycolysis, aerobic respiration) and state how long each can sustain activity.
2
I will be able to describe at least three contributors to muscle fatigue and explain why fatigue is reversible.
3
I will be able to distinguish Type I, Type IIa, and Type IIx muscle fibers by speed, fatigue resistance, and best-suited activity.
