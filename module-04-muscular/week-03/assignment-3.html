📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 4.11 — Exercise Physiology: Hypertrophy, Atrophy, and Use
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 4.11 and complete the activities in Lesson 4.11.
Lesson focus: Muscles adapt to what you ask of them. So does the body you will live in.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment closes the loop between the molecular biology of the muscle and the everyday biology of training and aging. You will compare resistance training and endurance training, explain atrophy, describe sarcopenia, and reflect on what you have learned.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If sarcopenia is largely unavoidable, why is consistent resistance training still the strongest evidence-based intervention against it?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Resistance vs endurance table. Two columns (resistance training, endurance training). At least three cellular / structural changes per column. Photo from your notebook is fine.
2
Hypertrophy mechanism (one paragraph). Explain in your own words how a muscle gets bigger from heavy training. Use the words satellite cells, myofibrils, and protein synthesis. State explicitly whether new muscle fibers are formed. 3–5 sentences.
3
Atrophy types (3 short bullets). Briefly describe disuse atrophy, denervation atrophy, and age-related atrophy. For each, state whether it is reversible and under what condition.
4
Focus-question answer (4–6 sentences). Answer the focus question. Address (a) what sarcopenia is, (b) what resistance training does at the cellular level that pushes back against it, and (c) why “unavoidable” does not mean “hopeless.”
5
Total length: comparison table + 350–500 words.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Comparison table contains at least three accurate changes per training type.
☐
Hypertrophy paragraph correctly states that fibers thicken, not multiply; satellite cells, myofibrils, and protein synthesis all named correctly.
☐
All three atrophy types correctly described and reversibility correctly attributed.
☐
Sarcopenia answer reflects honest hedging: a slowable but not stoppable process; resistance training is the strongest documented intervention.
