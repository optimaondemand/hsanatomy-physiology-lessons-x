Module thread — Self-government. The lower limb is the part of the body that obeys most directly when you decide to go somewhere. Self-government begins below the waist.

Reading 4.09 — Muscles of the Pelvis and Lower Limb

Before you start the activities below, open Reading 4.09 in Canvas and read it end-to-end.
🥽 YOUR VR SESSION THIS WEEK
Trace these muscles onto the figure:
▶ Gluteus maximus (red) — the buttock; trace both sides.
▶ Iliopsoas (dark red) — on a small patch with the abdominal wall made transparent, trace the psoas major descending from the lumbar spine and the iliacus from the inside of the ilium, merging into one tendon crossing the front of the hip.
▶ Quadriceps femoris (orange) — trace all four heads visibly on the right thigh (rectus femoris in the middle, vastus lateralis to the outside, vastus medialis to the inside, with vastus intermedius indicated as “hidden beneath”). Converge them into a single patellar tendon over the patella to the tibial tuberosity.
▶ Hamstrings (yellow) — on the right back of thigh, trace biceps femoris (lateral), semitendinosus (medial superficial), semimembranosus (medial deep) from ischial tuberosity down to their tibial / fibular insertions.
▶ Tibialis anterior (light green) — trace along the outer edge of the shin from the lateral tibial condyle to the top of the foot.
▶ Gastrocnemius (green) — trace the two heads of the calf on the back of the lower leg, converging into the Achilles tendon at the calcaneus.
▶ Soleus (blue) — on a small patch with the gastrocnemius made transparent, trace the soleus underneath. Note that the soleus does not cross the knee.
Label every muscle / head. Restore the gastrocnemius transparency before saving. Save your work.
Time: ~36 minutes.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name and locate the gluteus maximus, the iliopsoas, the four quadriceps muscles, the three hamstrings, the tibialis anterior, and the two calf muscles.
2
I will be able to state the action of each muscle / group and identify the antagonist pair at the hip, knee, and ankle.
3
I will be able to distinguish the gastrocnemius from the soleus by which joints each crosses.
4
I will be able to explain why the patella is described as a sesamoid bone embedded in the quadriceps tendon, and why shin splints are linked to the tibialis anterior.
