Module 4 Writing Assignment
From Nerve Impulse to Muscle Fiber Shortening
Analytical Essay · 30 pts · Due end of Module 4 (Week 3)
Module thread — Self-government. Every voluntary contraction in your body runs through the same molecular chain of events. Knowing the chain is knowing what it costs your body to do anything at all.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Module 4 has taught you the anatomy of the muscular system from the molecular level (the sarcomere) up to the whole-body level (every major muscle on the figure). This module-level writing assignment asks you to trace the physiology from end to end — the chain of events that begins when a motor neuron decides to fire and ends when an individual muscle fiber shortens. You will name the role of calcium and ATP at each step, and you will explain in your own words why the contraction stops when stimulation ends.
✏️ THE PROMPT
Muscle contraction depends on a precise sequence of molecular events, from the arrival of a nerve impulse at the neuromuscular junction to the sliding of filaments inside a sarcomere. Trace the chain of events from action potential at the neuromuscular junction to muscle fiber shortening. Identify the role of calcium ions and ATP at each step. Then explain — in your own words — why a muscle stops contracting when stimulation ends.
Length: 500–700 words.
📝 WHAT A STRONG ESSAY INCLUDES
1
Step 1 — the neuromuscular junction. Open with the arrival of the action potential at the axon terminal. Describe the sequence: voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open, vesicles fuse and release ACh, ACh diffuses across the synaptic cleft, ACh binds to receptors on the motor end plate, Na⁺ enters the muscle fiber, an end-plate potential triggers an action potential in the sarcolemma. Use the word acetylcholine.
2
Step 2 — carrying the signal into the fiber. The muscle action potential runs along the sarcolemma and into the T-tubules. At the triads, voltage sensors trigger the sarcoplasmic reticulum to release stored Ca²⁺ into the cytoplasm around the sarcomeres. Name the structures.
3
Step 3 — the calcium gate opens. Calcium binds to troponin. Troponin shifts tropomyosin off the myosin-binding sites on the thin filament. Actin’s binding sites are now exposed. (This is the role of Ca²⁺.)
4
Step 4 — the cross-bridge cycle. Walk through the four stages: cocked myosin head (with ADP and Pi) → head binds actin (cross-bridge formed) → Pi and ADP released, power stroke pulls the thin filament toward the M-line → new ATP binds, head detaches and re-cocks. State the two distinct roles of ATP — hydrolysis cocks the head; binding releases the head.
5
Step 5 — how shortening happens. Thousands of cross-bridge cycles per second across millions of sarcomeres pull the thin filaments inward toward the M-line. The Z-discs come along with them, the sarcomere shortens, the I-band and the H-zone narrow, the A-band stays the same. The whole fiber shortens.
6
Step 6 — why contraction stops. Close by explaining what happens when the motor neuron stops firing. Acetylcholinesterase breaks down ACh in the cleft within milliseconds. No new end-plate potentials. The SR’s calcium pumps clear Ca²⁺ back into the SR. Tropomyosin slides back over the binding sites. Cross-bridges break. The muscle relaxes. Be explicit that relaxation is also an active process — the SR pumps run on ATP.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All six steps in the chain are present, in correct order.
☐
The role of Ca²⁺ is specifically identified at the triad and again at the troponin / tropomyosin gate.
☐
The two distinct roles of ATP are correctly identified: hydrolysis cocks the head, binding releases it. Rigor mortis may be referenced as evidence.
☐
Anatomical terminology used correctly: neuromuscular junction, acetylcholine, sarcolemma, T-tubule, sarcoplasmic reticulum, troponin, tropomyosin, sarcomere, Z-disc, actin, myosin, cross-bridge, M-line, acetylcholinesterase.
☐
The “why does contraction stop” closing explicitly names the role of acetylcholinesterase and the SR pumps.
☐
Length: 500–700 words.
