Reading 4.06 — Muscles of the Back and Thorax
The substantive content for this lesson lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before you start the activities below, open Reading 4.06 in Canvas and read it end-to-end — it covers both regions in one pass.
✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK — BACK, THEN THORAX
This is a two-part drawing session: the back first, then the front of the chest. Start a new page, “Muscle Map — Back,” with a large back-view body outline, and keep your front-view Muscle Map from Lesson 4.01 beside it for Part 2.
Part 1 — the back.
▶ Trapezius (orange) — draw the diamond from occipital protuberance + C7–T12 spinous processes to clavicle, acromion, and spine of scapula.
▶ Latissimus dorsi (red) — draw from T7–L5 spinous processes + iliac crest sweeping up to its insertion on the front of the humerus.
▶ Show the deeper layer with a cut-away window: draw a dashed outline over the middle of the back where the trapezius and latissimus are “peeled back,” and draw the erector spinae inside that window (the superficial muscles stay drawn around the window).
▶ Erector spinae — iliocostalis (yellow, lateral column), longissimus (green, middle column, running to the mastoid process), spinalis (blue, medial column, against the spinous processes).
▶ Label the window “trapezius & latissimus peeled back.”
Part 2 — the thorax. Turn to your front-view outline.
▶ External intercostals (light blue) — draw the diagonal fibers (down and forward) across 3–4 rib spaces on the right side of the chest.
▶ Internal intercostals (dark blue) — draw the perpendicular fibers (down and backward) across the same rib spaces on the left side, so you can compare directions side-by-side.
▶ Diaphragm (purple) — draw a cut-away window through the front of the chest wall and draw the dome inside it. Indicate the central tendon and mark the three openings: caval (T8, right), esophageal (T10, slightly left of midline), aortic (T12, on the back).
Label every structure. Keep this page for your Muscle Map (photograph it or save the file).
Time: ~48 minutes.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name and locate the trapezius and latissimus dorsi, and describe the action each one produces.
2
I will be able to name the three columns of the erector spinae group in order from lateral to medial and explain why standing upright is “tonic” work.
3
I will be able to describe the action of the external and internal intercostals and identify each by fiber direction.
4
I will be able to locate the diaphragm, name its three openings and what passes through each, and describe what happens to the thoracic cavity when it contracts.

⚡ TODAY’S ACTIVITIES (~110 minutes total)
1
Read Reading 4.06
30 min
Read end-to-end. Pay attention to the layer-on-layer description in the back section and the fiber-direction contrast in the thorax section.
2
OIA table + breathing diagram
18 min
Make a 4-row OIA table: trapezius, latissimus dorsi, erector spinae (as one row, naming iliocostalis/longissimus/spinalis in the notes cell), diaphragm. Then sketch the thorax in side view, twice: once with the diaphragm dome-shaped (rest), once flattened (inhalation). Indicate the volume change.
3
Drawing session — back, then thorax
48 min
Follow the green drawing-session callout, both parts. Make sure the intercostal fibers point in clearly different directions on the left and right sides.
4
Felt anatomy — posture and breath
10 min
Sit or stand up straight and notice which back muscle you suspect is doing the most work in your current posture. Then place one hand on your upper belly and breathe slowly — as you inhale, your belly should push outward (the diaphragm flattening and pushing the abdominal organs forward). Write 2–3 sentences covering both observations.

Complete the Lesson 4.06 Assignment
