📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 4.06 — Muscles of the Back and Thorax
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 4.06 and complete the activities in Lesson 4.06.
Lesson focus: Deep back muscles hold the spine vertical and the diaphragm drives breathing — both run in the background, without being asked.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This is a double-length atlas assignment covering both regions from Lesson 4.06: an OIA table for the back and the diaphragm, a labeled photo of your Muscle Map showing both regions, and one combined focus question about posture and breathing.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Your erector spinae group and your diaphragm are both muscles that work continuously without you deciding to fire them. What do they have in common, and what is different about how each one does its job?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
OIA table. Four rows: trapezius, latissimus dorsi, erector spinae (treat the group as one row, but name iliocostalis, longissimus, and spinalis in the “notes” cell from lateral to medial), diaphragm (note its three openings and what passes through each in the “notes” cell).
2
Labeled Muscle Map photo(s). A photo of your back view showing trapezius and latissimus in their colors with the three erector spinae columns visible (use a cut-away window or take two photos), and a photo of your front view showing the intercostal fiber directions on both sides and the diaphragm with its three openings labeled.
3
Posture-and-breath answer (6–8 sentences). Answer the focus question. Address (a) what muscle tone actually is at the motor-unit level — a rotating subset of motor units firing at any instant, not the whole muscle contracting nonstop — and why that prevents the erector spinae from fatiguing, (b) how this differs from the diaphragm's job, which is not tonic at all but a full contract/relax rhythm driven by the brainstem, and (c) why sitting hunched forward at a screen for hours affects one of these muscles more than the other.
4
Total length: OIA table + Muscle Map photo(s) + 200–300 words.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All four rows of OIA table accurate; erector spinae columns named in order; diaphragm openings paired correctly to their structures.
☐
Muscle Map photo(s) show all named back muscles and thorax structures, correctly positioned and labeled.
☐
Posture-and-breath answer explains muscle tone as rotating motor-unit recruitment (not constant whole-muscle contraction), distinguishes it from the diaphragm’s rhythmic breathing role, and connects both to a real scenario.
