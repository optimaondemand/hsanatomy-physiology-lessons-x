📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 4.05 — Muscles of the Head and Neck
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 4.05 and complete the activities in Lesson 4.05, including the drawing session.
Lesson focus: When a muscle contracts, it pulls its insertion toward its origin.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This is the first of six “muscle atlas” assignments in Module 4. The structure is the same each time. (1) Submit your origin-insertion-action (OIA) table for the muscles of this lesson’s body region. (2) Submit a labeled photo of your Muscle Map page. (3) Answer one functional-reasoning question that ties the region’s muscles to a real action.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why is the sternocleidomastoid one muscle but two attachments at its origin, and what does it let the head do that a single-origin muscle could not?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
OIA table. Submit the 8-row table from your lesson activity: muscle · origin · insertion · action. Confirm all eight muscles (masseter, temporalis, orbicularis oculi, orbicularis oris, frontalis, zygomaticus major, buccinator, sternocleidomastoid). Photo of notebook is acceptable.
2
Labeled Muscle Map photo. Submit a photo (or digital export) of your Muscle Map page with all eight muscles drawn and labeled in their assigned colors.
3
Focus-question answer (3–5 sentences). Answer the focus question: why two origins on the SCM? Address (a) the two origins (sternum + clavicle) and the single insertion (mastoid process), (b) what happens when one side contracts vs both, and (c) one everyday action that depends on this design.
4
Total length: OIA table + Muscle Map photo + 100–200 words of writing. Submit as Canvas text entry with image attachments, or as a single .docx / .pdf.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All 8 muscles named correctly in the OIA table; origins, insertions, and actions match Reading 4.05.
☐
Muscle Map photo shows all 8 muscles in their assigned colors with visible labels.
☐
Focus-question answer addresses one-side vs two-side contraction and connects to a real action.
