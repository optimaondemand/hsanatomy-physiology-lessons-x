Module thread — Self-government. Most of what your hand does, you decide. Most of what your hand can do depends on muscles that begin near your elbow.
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Reading 4.08 — Muscles of the Upper Limb
The substantive content for this lesson lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before you start the activities below, open Reading 4.08 in Canvas and read it end-to-end.
✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK
Draw these muscles in order onto your Muscle Map (front and back views):
▶ Deltoid (red) — the rounded cap of the shoulder; draw it on both sides if time allows, but at least the right side.
▶ Biceps brachii (orange) — front of upper arm; draw clearly the two heads at the top, merging into one belly, inserting on the radial tuberosity.
▶ Triceps brachii (yellow) — back of upper arm; draw converging on the olecranon process.
▶ Forearm flexors (light blue) — draw the anterior compartment of the forearm as a single block. Add a small marker showing the common origin on the medial epicondyle of the humerus.
▶ Forearm extensors (dark blue) — draw the posterior compartment as a single block. Add a marker on the lateral epicondyle for the common origin.
Label every muscle / group. Keep this page for your Muscle Map (photograph it or save the file).
Time: ~30 minutes.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name the deltoid, biceps brachii, and triceps brachii and state the origin, insertion, and primary action of each.
2
I will be able to distinguish the forearm flexor compartment from the forearm extensor compartment by location, origin epicondyle, and action.
3
I will be able to identify the biceps and triceps as an antagonist pair and explain why every joint needs muscles on both sides.
4
I will be able to connect the medial and lateral epicondyles of the humerus (Module 3) to the muscle attachments that depend on them.
