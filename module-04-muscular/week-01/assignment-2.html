📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 4.02 — Skeletal Muscle Microscopic Anatomy
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 4.02 and complete the activities in Lesson 4.02, including the VR Skeletal Muscle Sculpt.
Lesson focus: A skeletal muscle nests from whole muscle down to myofibril; T-tubules and the sarcoplasmic reticulum carry the trigger signal in.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has two parts: an infographic showing the nested hierarchy of a skeletal muscle, from whole muscle down to the sarcoplasmic reticulum, and a short written explanation of how a contraction signal travels from the surface of a muscle fiber to its interior. Both are drawn from Reading 4.02 and from your VR Skeletal Muscle Sculpt.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
A muscle fiber can be 30 centimeters long. How does the signal to contract reach the interior of the fiber within a fraction of a second?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Hierarchy infographic. Submit either a photograph of your hand-drawn macro-to-microscopic diagram (from the lesson activity) or a screenshot/snapshot of the muscle you built in VR. Your infographic must label all nine parts, in order: whole muscle, epimysium, fascicle, perimysium, muscle fiber, endomysium, T-tubule, myofibril, sarcoplasmic reticulum.
2
Signal-path paragraph (5–8 sentences). Answer the focus question. Trace the path of the contraction signal from the sarcolemma, through a T-tubule, to a terminal cisterna of the sarcoplasmic reticulum, to the release of Ca²⁺ toward the myofibrils. Use the word triad at least once. Explain why a fiber that long would not work without T-tubules.
3
One-sentence T-tubule note. In one sentence, state in your own words what Andrew Huxley and Robert Taylor’s 1958 local-activation experiment proved about T-tubules.
4
Total length: infographic + 150–250 words of writing. Submit as Canvas text entry plus image attachment, or as a .docx / .pdf with the image embedded.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All nine hierarchy parts labeled and correctly placed on the infographic.
☐
Signal path traced from sarcolemma to Ca²⁺ release; the word triad used correctly.
☐
Reasoning given for why T-tubules are necessary in a long fiber.
☐
T-tubule note is in the student’s own words and identifies that the experiment showed T-tubules carry the signal, not just that they exist.
