✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK — BUILD A MUSCLE FROM THE INSIDE OUT
This is your first detail drawing of Module 4. Instead of drawing the sarcomere (that comes next lesson), you draw the muscle around it — from the inside out, one layer at a time, at exaggerated scale, as a nested cross-section on its own detail page that sits beside your Muscle Map. (This is a cross-section, so it is different from the lengthwise macro-to-microscopic diagram in Activity 2.)
What to do. On a fresh page (paper highly recommended), draw a cross-section that builds outward in this order, starting with a small circle at the center and adding each wrapper as a larger ring around the last:
▶ Draw one small circle — a single myofibril, seen in cross-section.
▶ Draw a fine dotted outline around it — the sarcoplasmic reticulum. Add a couple of short perpendicular tunnels touching the mesh to represent T-tubules.
▶ Group 6–8 of these wrapped myofibrils together inside a slightly larger circle — this bundle, with its own thin membrane (the sarcolemma) and a few nuclei dotted along the outside, is one muscle fiber.
▶ Draw a thin ring around the fiber: the endomysium.
▶ Group 4–5 wrapped fibers together and draw a second ring around that group — the perimysium. This bundle is one fascicle.
▶ Group several fascicles together and draw a final, thicker ring around the whole thing — the epimysium. You have built one whole muscle. Label every structure you drew.
Keep this page for your Muscle Map (photograph it or save the file). Next lesson you draw the sarcomere itself, inside a myofibril, and step it through a contraction.
Time: ~30 minutes.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to describe the nested structure of a skeletal muscle from whole muscle to sarcomere, naming the three connective-tissue sheaths (epimysium, perimysium, endomysium).
2
I will be able to label the nine-part hierarchy of a skeletal muscle from whole muscle down to the sarcoplasmic reticulum: epimysium, fascicle, perimysium, muscle fiber, endomysium, myofibril, T-tubule, sarcolemma, sarcoplasmic reticulum.
3
I will be able to explain what T-tubules and the sarcoplasmic reticulum do, including the role of the triad in carrying the contraction signal.
4
I will be able to describe how Andrew Huxley and Robert Taylor's 1958 local-activation experiment proved that T-tubules carry the contraction signal into the fiber.
