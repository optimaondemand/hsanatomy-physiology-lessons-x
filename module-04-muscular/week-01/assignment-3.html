Lesson focus: Filaments slide; nothing inside the sarcomere shortens.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment locks in the cross-bridge cycle. You will (1) put the four stages of the cycle in order, (2) state the two roles of ATP, (3) explain the role of calcium, and (4) predict what happens to the band widths during contraction.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why does a body that has just died become stiff — and then, hours later, become limp again?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Cycle in sequence. Write the four stages of the cross-bridge cycle in order, each in one sentence. Stage labels you must use: cocked head, cross-bridge formation, power stroke, head release / re-cock. Beside each stage, note what ATP, ADP, or Pi is doing.
2
Two roles of ATP (one paragraph). Distinguish what ATP hydrolysis does from what ATP binding does. Then use this distinction to answer the focus question: explain rigor mortis — both why the body stiffens shortly after death and why the stiffness eventually goes away (hint: protein breakdown). 4–6 sentences.
3
The calcium gate (one paragraph). Describe the role of Ca²⁺, troponin, and tropomyosin at rest and during contraction. Use the words tropomyosin, troponin, and binding site at least once each. 3–5 sentences.
4
Band-width prediction. List the I-band, the A-band, and the H-zone. For each, state whether it gets narrower, wider, or does not change during contraction. Add one sentence justifying the A-band prediction specifically.
5
Total length: 300–500 words. Submit as Canvas text entry or as a .docx / .pdf.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Four cycle stages in correct order; ATP/ADP/Pi mentioned at the right step.
☐

☐

☐
