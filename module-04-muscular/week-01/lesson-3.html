🥽 YOUR VR SESSION THIS WEEK — SCULPT, THEN ANIMATE
This is the sarcomere session. You will sculpt one simplified sarcomere — the contractile unit at the heart of every skeletal muscle — at exaggerated scale, off-body in open space, then animate it: step the thin filaments inward through one cycle so the sarcomere visibly contracts. This sarcomere is the “off-body detail” required in your end-of-module snapshot.
Part 1 — sculpt. Open ENGAGE. In an open space (not on the figure), use the 3D pen tool to sculpt one sarcomere, in this order:
▶ Draw two parallel Z-discs as vertical lines, about an arm’s width apart.
▶ From each Z-disc, draw 4–6 parallel thin filaments (actin) reaching toward the center, leaving a central gap.
▶ In the central region, sculpt 3–5 thicker, parallel thick filaments (myosin), overlapping the thin filaments on both sides but leaving a gap in the very middle (the H-zone). Draw a vertical M-line through their center.
▶ Place labels on: Z-disc (both), thin filament, thick filament, A-band, I-band, H-zone, M-line.
Part 2 — animate. Take a snapshot of the relaxed sarcomere first.
▶ Mark a starting state. Note the width of the I-band and the H-zone in your notebook (you do not need exact measurements — just “wide,” “medium,” or “narrow”).
▶ Add a few myosin heads. On the thick filaments, sculpt 4–6 short stubs angling out toward the thin filaments. These are the cross-bridges.
▶ Step 1 of the cycle. Connect a myosin head stub to the nearest spot on a thin filament. Take a snapshot. Label: “cross-bridge formed.”
▶ Step 2 — the power stroke. Move the thin filament a short distance toward the M-line. Move the Z-disc at that end of the sarcomere the same distance inward. Take a snapshot. Label: “power stroke — sarcomere shortens.”
▶ Step 3 — head releases, re-cocks. Break the connection. Re-orient the head stub back to its starting angle. Snapshot, label: “ATP releases & re-cocks head.”
▶ Step 4 — repeat once more. Reattach the head one binding-site farther along the thin filament. Power-stroke again. Compare the final I-band and H-zone width to the starting state.
Save the snapshot sequence. The four-frame story is your record of how a single cross-bridge cycle moves the sarcomere.
Time: ~45 minutes.

1
I will be able to label the parts of a sarcomere: Z-discs, A-band, I-band, H-zone, M-line, thick (myosin) filaments, thin (actin) filaments, and describe what Hugh Huxley and Andrew Huxley contributed to our understanding of how contraction works.
2
I will be able to state, in order, the four stages of the cross-bridge cycle.
3
I will be able to distinguish the two roles of ATP in muscle contraction (hydrolysis vs. binding) and explain rigor mortis on that basis.
4
I will be able to describe the role of calcium, troponin, and tropomyosin in opening and closing the actin binding sites.
5
I will be able to predict how the I-band, H-zone, and A-band widths change during contraction.
