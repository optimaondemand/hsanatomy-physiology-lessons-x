📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 4.01 — Three Muscle Types: Skeletal, Smooth, and Cardiac
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 4.01 and complete the activities in Lesson 4.01, including the Muscle Map orientation.
Lesson focus: Three muscle types share one chemical superpower but live in different places and answer to different commanders.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
You read about the three muscle types and placed one example of each on your Muscle Map. In this assignment you will (1) produce a comparison chart of the three types, (2) classify five everyday body actions by the muscle type that drives them, and (3) write a short reflection on why the body needs three different muscle types instead of one.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why does the body use three different muscle types instead of one?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Comparison chart. Make a 3-column chart (skeletal / smooth / cardiac) with six rows: striated (yes/no), voluntary (yes/no), cell shape, nuclei per cell, one special feature, one location in the body. Complete every cell. Photograph or scan from your notebook is fine.
2
Classify five actions. For each action below, name the muscle type responsible and the location in the body where the responsible muscle lives. (a) running uphill, (b) the heart pumping blood, (c) the iris of the eye narrowing in bright light, (d) blinking your eyelid, (e) food being pushed through the small intestine.
3
Final reflection (3–5 sentences). Answer the focus question: why does the body use three different muscle types instead of one? Tie your answer to at least two specific features that differ between the types (e.g., voluntary vs. involuntary; fatigues vs. does not fatigue).
4
Total length: 200–350 words (plus the chart). Submit as a Canvas text entry, or attach a .docx / .pdf.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Comparison chart complete; all 18 cells filled with accurate information.
☐
All five actions classified correctly with a body location for each.
☐
Reflection ties “why three types?” to at least two specific differences — does not just restate the chart.
☐
Anatomical terminology used correctly (no “smooth muscle in the heart” etc.).
