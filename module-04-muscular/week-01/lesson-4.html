🥽 YOUR VR SESSION THIS WEEK
This week you sculpt a simplified neuromuscular junction at exaggerated scale in open space (off-body). This is your second off-body detail for the Module 4 snapshot — alongside the sarcomere from Lesson 4.03.
What to do. Open ENGAGE. In open space, sculpt the NMJ in three parts:
▶ Motor neuron axon terminal (presynaptic). Sculpt a swollen bulb at the end of a thin axon. Inside the bulb, sculpt 8–15 small spheres — these are your synaptic vesicles packed with ACh. Indicate one vesicle that is fused with the membrane and one that is in mid-release.
▶ Synaptic cleft. Leave an obvious gap between the terminal and the next structure. Sprinkle a few dots in the gap to represent ACh that has just been released and is on its way across.
▶ Motor end plate (postsynaptic). Sculpt a folded membrane on the muscle fiber side — suggest the folds with a wavy line. Studded along the membrane, sculpt 6–10 small “receptors” (tiny cylinders or cubes). Indicate one or two as “open” with arrows showing Na⁺ entering the muscle fiber.
▶ Place labels on: axon terminal, synaptic vesicle, ACh in cleft, synaptic cleft, motor end plate, ACh receptor, Na⁺ entry arrow.
Save the file.
Time: ~30 minutes.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name the three parts of the neuromuscular junction (axon terminal, synaptic cleft, motor end plate) and describe what each contains.
2
I will be able to state the six steps of synaptic transmission at the NMJ in order, from action potential at the terminal to acetylcholinesterase break-down of ACh.
3
I will be able to identify acetylcholine as the neurotransmitter of the skeletal NMJ and connect it to Otto Loewi’s 1921 experiment.
4
I will be able to explain why blocking ACh release (botulinum toxin) or blocking ACh break-down (anti-cholinesterase drugs) produces opposite kinds of muscle problems.
