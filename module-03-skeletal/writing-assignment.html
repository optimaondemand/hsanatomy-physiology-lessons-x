Module 3 Writing Assignment
Synovial Joints — Form, Function, and What Breaks
Analytical Essay · 30 pts · Due end of Module 3 (Week 3)
Module thread — Perseverance. The synovial joints in your body are engineered to last a lifetime under load. Understanding what makes them work — and what makes them fail — is one of the most useful things you will learn this module.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Module 3 has taught you the anatomy of the skeleton. This assignment asks you to combine that anatomy with the matching physiology — how the joint actually works under load. You will choose one synovial joint, explain how its structure (form) enables its specific function, and then describe what happens when that structure breaks down. This is the kind of writing you will do every module of this year.
✏️ THE PROMPT
Synovial joints permit a remarkable range of motion while sustaining the loads of daily life. Choose one synovial joint — knee, shoulder, or hip — and explain how its structure (form) enables its specific function. Then describe what goes wrong when that structure breaks down due to injury, age-related wear, or disease.
Use anatomical terminology accurately. Cite at least one specific structural feature (e.g., a particular ligament, the labrum, the menisci, the articular cartilage) by name.
Length: 500–700 words.
📝 WHAT A STRONG ESSAY INCLUDES
1
An anatomical orientation paragraph. Open by stating which joint you have chosen. Name the two articulating bones, the type of synovial joint it is (hinge, ball-and-socket, etc.), and the general range of motion it allows.
2
A form-follows-function section. Walk through the joint’s key structural features and tie each to a functional consequence. Name at least one specific feature by its anatomical name (e.g., the menisci of the knee, the labrum of the shoulder, the ligamentum teres of the hip).
3
A breakdown section. Describe one specific failure mode of your chosen joint — injury (e.g., ACL tear), age-related wear (e.g., osteoarthritis), or disease (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis). Name the structure that fails and explain how its failure compromises the joint’s function.
4
A closing paragraph that returns to the form-follows-function idea. Why do the joints that move the most often fail in such characteristic ways?
💡 PER-JOINT STARTERS (use ONE)
Knee (hinge joint, modified):
Articulating bones: femur + tibia (patella anteriorly). Specific features to consider: medial and lateral menisci; anterior and posterior cruciate ligaments (ACL and PCL); medial and lateral collateral ligaments. Common failures: ACL tear (sports injury), torn meniscus, osteoarthritis of the joint surfaces.
Shoulder (ball-and-socket):
Articulating bones: humerus + scapula (at the glenoid cavity). Specific features to consider: the shallow glenoid cavity; the glenoid labrum (a fibrous rim that deepens the socket); the rotator cuff group (a set of muscles + tendons that surround the joint). Common failures: shoulder dislocation (humerus pops out of the glenoid), rotator cuff tear, labral tear.
Hip (ball-and-socket):
Articulating bones: femur + os coxa (at the acetabulum). Specific features to consider: the deep acetabulum; the acetabular labrum; the strong iliofemoral ligament; the ligamentum teres (a small ligament inside the joint that contains a tiny artery to the femoral head). Common failures: hip fracture at the femoral neck (osteoporosis), labral tear, osteoarthritis.
