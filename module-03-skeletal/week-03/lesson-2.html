Before you start the activities below, open Reading 3.10 in Canvas and read it end-to-end.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to distinguish the tibia and fibula by size and weight-bearing role.
2
I will be able to identify the medial and lateral malleoli and connect each to its parent bone.
3
I will be able to name the talus and calcaneus and explain their distinct roles in standing and walking.
4
I will be able to describe the arches of the foot and explain their shock-absorbing function.

Footprint sketch
15 min
1
Trace your right foot onto a notebook page.
2
Inside the outline, sketch in: tibia (cut off at the top of the outline), fibula (cut off at the top), talus, calcaneus (heel), other tarsals, the 5 metatarsals, and the 14 phalanges. Use bones as guides, not anatomical perfection.
3
Draw a curving line along the inside of your foot to indicate the medial longitudinal arch.
✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK
This week finishes the lower limb bones. 26 small bones in the ankle and foot alone — the foundation of walking.
▶ Take a fresh page (paper highly recommended; a digital drawing file is also fine), title it with this lesson’s region as the next page of your Skeleton Atlas, and use the figures in this week’s reading as your reference. Draw each bone large enough to label.
▶ Draw the right tibia (yellow). Label the medial malleolus.
▶ Draw the right fibula (pink). Label the lateral malleolus.
▶ Draw the right patella.
▶ Draw all 7 left tarsals (pink/magenta or equivalent bright color). Label the talus and the calcaneus.
▶ Draw all 5 right metatarsals and all 14 left phalanges of the foot.
Time: ~28 minutes.

Walking analysis
8 min
1
Walk slowly across the room, paying attention to which part of your foot touches the ground first, which part bears weight in the middle of the step, and which part pushes off last.
2
Write three sentences in your notebook describing the sequence of bony contact during one step. Name at least one specific bone in each sentence (heel = calcaneus; arch = metatarsals; push-off = phalanges).
