Reading 3.11 — Joints: Where Bones Meet

🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to classify a joint functionally (synarthrosis / amphiarthrosis / diarthrosis) and structurally (fibrous / cartilaginous / synovial).
2
I will be able to name the six features of a synovial joint and describe what each contributes.
3
I will be able to identify the six types of synovial joint (plane, hinge, pivot, condyloid, saddle, ball-and-socket) and give an example of each.
4
I will be able to explain why articular cartilage is difficult to regrow once damaged.

🥽 YOUR VR SESSION THIS WEEK
This week you trace a synovial joint in detail. You will use this joint for your module writing assignment, so choose carefully — knee, shoulder, or hip.
▶ Open ENGAGE and load HoloAnatomy (male figure). Choose one of: knee, shoulder, or hip.
▶ Trace the two articulating bones at that joint (e.g., femur and tibia at the knee).
▶ Trace and label the articular cartilage covering the bone ends.
▶ Trace and label at least two major ligaments at the joint (e.g., ACL + PCL at the knee, or rotator-cuff ligaments at the shoulder).
▶ Trace and label the joint capsule.
▶ If you chose the knee, also identify the medial and lateral menisci.
Time: ~25 minutes. The joint you sculpt today appears in your module-end snapshot.

VR synovial joint tracing

Follow the VR callout above. Choose knee, shoulder, or hip. Trace both bones, articular cartilage, two ligaments, and the joint capsule. (If knee: include menisci.)
4
Synovial joint cross-section
15 min
1
Sketch a cross-section of a generic synovial joint in your notebook. Label all six features: articular cartilage, joint cavity, articular capsule (with synovial membrane), synovial fluid, ligaments, and sensory nerves (an arrow pointing into the capsule).
5
Movement breakdown
15 min
1
Pick one everyday or sporting movement: kicking a soccer ball, serving a volleyball, or jumping rope. (You may safely mimic the movement in place, or simply visualize it in slow motion.)
2
Break the movement down into 6 specific joint movements, using the vocabulary from the reading’s callout (flexion/extension, abduction/adduction, circumduction, rotation, elevation/depression, pronation/supination, dorsiflexion/plantarflexion, opposition/reposition).
3
For each of the 6, name the specific joint involved — not just a body region. “Wrist” is ambiguous (it could mean the radiocarpal joint or an intercarpal joint, and those allow very different motions); name the specific joint whenever you can.
4
Record your 6-movement breakdown in your notebook as a numbered list: movement type → specific joint → one clause on what the movement accomplishes.

Complete the Lesson 3.11 Assignment
