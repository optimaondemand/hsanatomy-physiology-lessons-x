📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 3.11 — Joints: Where Bones Meet
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 3.11 and complete the activities in Lesson 3.11.
Lesson focus: Joint architecture controls range of motion; synovial joints are the most freely movable.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Submit the joint-type table, the synovial-joint cross-section sketch, and the movement-breakdown list from Lesson 3.11. This is also your direct lead-in to the M3 module writing assignment — the joint you drew today is the joint you will analyze there.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why does an injured joint heal so slowly compared to a fractured bone?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Synovial joint type table. Submit your 6-row table from Lesson 3.11. Each row: joint type + body example + motion described.
2
Synovial joint cross-section sketch. Submit your labeled diagram showing all six features (articular cartilage, joint cavity, articular capsule + synovial membrane, synovial fluid, ligaments, sensory nerves).
3
Focus-question response (5–7 sentences). Why does cartilage heal so poorly? Use the avascular nature of articular cartilage and the lack of blood-borne repair cells in your explanation. Contrast with the rich blood supply of bone and what that allows.
4
Movement breakdown list. Submit your 6-movement breakdown from Lesson 3.11 (a soccer kick, volleyball serve, jumping rope, or similar). Each of the 6 lines must name a specific joint movement type and a specific joint (e.g. “radiocarpal” rather than just “wrist”) — not just a joint region.
5
Total length: 350–500 words plus the table, sketch, and movement list.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Table includes all 6 synovial joint types with correct examples and motion described.
☐
Cross-section sketch labels all 6 features of a synovial joint.
☐
Focus-question response addresses avascular cartilage and contrasts with bone’s vascular repair.
☐
Movement breakdown names 6 specific joint movements, each tied to a specific joint (not just a region).
