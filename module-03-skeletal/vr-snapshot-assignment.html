The Assembled Skeleton
VR submission · 25 pts · Due end of Module 3 (Week 3)
Module thread — Perseverance. Three weeks of lesson-by-lesson sculpting now come together in a single figure. This submission is the visible proof that you built the skeleton piece by piece.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
During this module you have sculpted individual bones lesson by lesson in VR. For this assignment, you reassemble all of your work into a complete skeleton, capture the snapshot in ENGAGE, and submit the IFX file along with a brief written caption. This is your proof that you have made the figure your own — not a finished textbook diagram, but a record of your own iterative building over three weeks.
🧸 EXPECTED STRUCTURES IN YOUR SNAPSHOT
Your snapshot must show the assembled skeleton with both the axial and appendicular components, plus one detailed synovial joint. Specifically:
Axial skeleton
▶
Skull (cranial + facial bones + hyoid). Dentition optional but encouraged.
▶
Full vertebral column (7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, sacrum, coccyx) with intervertebral discs visible.
▶
Sternum, 12 pairs of ribs, costal cartilages.
Appendicular skeleton
▶
Shoulder girdle (clavicle + scapula), upper arm (humerus), forearm (radius + ulna), wrist (8 carpals), palm (5 metacarpals), fingers (14 phalanges) — at least one side complete.
▶
Pelvic girdle (os coxa), thigh (femur), patella, lower leg (tibia + fibula), ankle (7 tarsals), foot (5 metatarsals + 14 phalanges) — at least one side complete.
Detailed synovial joint
▶
At least one synovial joint (knee, shoulder, or hip) with cartilage and at least two named ligaments visible. This is the joint you sculpted in Lesson 3.11 — the same joint you write about in the M3 Writing Assignment.
✏️ REFLECTION CAPTION (50–100 words)
Submit a brief written caption with your IFX that answers this reflection prompt (the same prompt posed in Lesson 3.12):
Over the past three weeks, we looked at both microscopic bone tissue and the whole skeleton. How has your view of bone changed from seeing it as “dead, dry rock” to seeing it as a living, dynamic organ?
Ground your answer in at least one specific structure from your snapshot (you can refer to the structure list above) — not just a general statement that bone is “alive,” but a concrete example of what that living quality actually does or looks like.
An audio caption is also acceptable in place of written, if your VR setup supports it.

This is a high school assignment, not a medical school anatomy practical. We are looking for evidence that you sculpted the skeleton piece by piece and reflect honestly on what you learned — not photorealistic accuracy.
