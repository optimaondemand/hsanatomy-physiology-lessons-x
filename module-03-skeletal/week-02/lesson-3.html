Module thread — Perseverance. The shoulder is the body’s most flexible joint and its most easily dislocated one. The body accepts a vulnerable bone for the gift of reach.
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Reading 3.07 — The Shoulder Girdle and the Upper Arm
The substantive content for this lesson lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before you start the activities below, open Reading 3.07 in Canvas and read it end-to-end.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name the bones of the shoulder girdle and identify the bony connection to the axial skeleton.
2
I will be able to locate the glenoid cavity, acromion, and coracoid process on the scapula.
3
I will be able to identify the head, surgical neck, and medial/lateral epicondyles of the humerus, and state what each is for.
4
I will be able to explain the mobility-stability trade-off at the shoulder joint.

2
Range-of-motion experiment
12 min
1
Stand up. Try to move your right arm in as many directions as you can: up, down, forward, back, across your chest, behind your back, in big circles. Count or sketch as many distinct directions as you find.
2
Try to move your right hip in the same directions. Compare. In your notebook, write one or two sentences about which joint is more mobile and which is more stable, based on what you just felt.
🥽 YOUR VR SESSION THIS WEEK
This week you start the appendicular skeleton. Trace the right-side shoulder girdle and upper arm.
▶ Open ENGAGE and load HoloAnatomy (male figure).
▶ Trace the right clavicle (pink). Identify both ends (sternoclavicular and acromioclavicular joints).
▶ Trace the right scapula (teal). Identify the glenoid cavity, acromion, and coracoid process.
▶ Trace the right humerus (yellow). Label the head, surgical neck, greater tubercle, and the medial and lateral epicondyles at the distal end.
