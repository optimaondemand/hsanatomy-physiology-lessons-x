The Thorax — Sternum, Ribs, and Costal Cartilages

Module thread — Perseverance. The rib cage moves more than ten thousand times a day — protective but never rigid. Every breath is the cage’s job.
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Reading 3.06 — The Thorax: Sternum, Ribs, and Costal Cartilages
The substantive content for this lesson lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before you start the activities below, open Reading 3.06 in Canvas and read it end-to-end.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name the three parts of the sternum and locate the sternal angle.
2
I will be able to distinguish true, false, and floating ribs by how each attaches anteriorly.
3
I will be able to explain why the rib cage needs cartilage and what would happen if costal cartilage were replaced by bone.

2
Breathing observation
10 min
1
Place one hand on your sternum. Take three slow breaths. Notice the sternum moves up and out on inhalation, not just up. Write one sentence describing what you observed.
2
Now place your fingers on the body of your sternum and notice the ridges that become palpable along either side — these are the costal cartilages that connect to your ribs. Feel along the sides of your rib cage to feel your ribs. You do not need to count to a specific rib; the point is simply to notice that the parts you are reading about are things you can find on your own body.
🥽 YOUR VR SESSION THIS WEEK
This week you complete the thoracic cage. The full axial skeleton (skull + spine + ribs + sternum) will be assembled and ready for the snapshot reassembly in Week 3.
▶ Open ENGAGE and load HoloAnatomy (male figure).
▶ Trace the sternum (pink). Identify and label the manubrium, body, and xiphoid process. Locate the sternal angle (where manubrium meets body).
▶ Trace all 12 pairs of ribs. Label rib 1 (true), rib 8 (false), and rib 11 (floating). Use different highlight colors for the three groups.
▶ Trace all costal cartilages (blue). Note how true ribs attach directly to the sternum, false ribs attach to rib 7’s cartilage, and ribs 11–12 float free anteriorly.
Time: ~22 minutes.
