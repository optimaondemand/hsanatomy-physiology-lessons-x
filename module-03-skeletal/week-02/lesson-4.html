🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to distinguish the radius from the ulna and explain how they enable pronation and supination.
2
I will be able to name the 8 carpal bones in their proximal and distal rows.
3
I will be able to count the bones in the hand correctly (5 metacarpals + 14 phalanges) and describe the saddle joint at the base of the thumb.
4
I will be able to describe the structure of the carpal tunnel and explain how carpal tunnel syndrome develops.

🥽 YOUR VR SESSION THIS WEEK
This is the most detailed VR session yet — 27 small bones in the hand and wrist alone. Work patiently. Save often.
▶ Open ENGAGE and load HoloAnatomy (male figure).
▶ Trace the right radius (blue) and right ulna (pink/magenta). Label the olecranon on the ulna and the head of the radius near the elbow, and the styloid process at the wrist end of each bone.
▶ Trace the 8 carpals of the right wrist as two rows of 4 (use two close but distinct colors to mark each row). Try to name each as you trace.
▶ Trace all 5 right metacarpals (pink/magenta) and all 14 right phalanges (yellow).
▶ Place one label on the carpal tunnel reading “median nerve passes here.”

4
Pronation / supination experiment
8 min
1
Hold your right arm out, palm up. With your left hand, place one finger on the head of the radius (lateral side, just below the elbow crease) and a second finger on the olecranon (the bony point at the back of your elbow).
2
Rotate your forearm palm-down (pronation), then back to palm-up (supination), slowly, keeping both fingers in place. Feel which of the two landmarks moves under your finger and which stays still.
3
Write one sentence answering: based on what you just felt, which bone is rotating (radius or ulna), and which bone stays put?
