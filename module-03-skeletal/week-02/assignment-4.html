📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 3.08 — The Forearm, Wrist, and Hand
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 3.08 and complete the activities in Lesson 3.08.
Lesson focus: 27 bones in each wrist and hand: the body invests heavily in the part of you that grips.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Submit your hand-trace diagram with all bones labeled, then write a short analysis of one specific action your hand can do because of its skeletal architecture.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Pick one specific hand action you do every day. Which bones, joints, and movements make it possible?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Hand-trace diagram. Submit a photo or scan of your labeled hand-trace diagram from Lesson 3.08. Required labels: radius, ulna (with their styloid processes), 8 carpals grouped by row (proximal row, distal row), 5 metacarpals, 14 phalanges, carpal tunnel.
2
Pronation / supination short answer (3 sentences). Describe what happens to the radius and the ulna when you turn your palm from facing up to facing down.
3
Focus-question reflection (5–7 sentences). Pick one specific hand action you do every day (typing, holding a pencil, throwing a ball, playing an instrument, brushing teeth, etc.). Name at least three specific bones and two specific joints the action uses. Explain how each contributes.
4
Total length: 300–450 words plus the diagram.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Hand diagram includes all required labels (radius, ulna, 8 carpals, 5 metacarpals, 14 phalanges, carpal tunnel).
☐
Pronation/supination response correctly identifies the radius as the moving bone.
☐
Reflection names a specific action plus 3 specific bones and 2 specific joints.
📊 GRADING RUBRIC
Criteria Excellent (4) Proficient (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1) Pts

Pronation/supination

Action reflection
4 pts		 3+ bones, 2+ joints, specific contributions. 3 bones, 2 joints; contributions general. 2 bones, 1 joint. Off-topic or vague. 4 pts
