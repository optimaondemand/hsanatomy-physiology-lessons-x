📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 3.01 — Bone Biology: Tissue, Shapes, and Markings
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 3.01 and complete the activities in Lesson 3.01, including the VR sculpting session.
Lesson focus: Bone is living tissue with characteristic shapes and surface markings, each shaped by function.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
In Reading 3.01 you learned that bone is living tissue, that the 206 bones of the skeleton fall into four shape categories (long, short, flat, irregular), and that every bony marking exists because something uses it. In this assignment you will produce a short set of field notes from your first VR sculpting session: a four-shape comparison table, an analysis of one bone marking, and a brief reflection on why form follows function in the skeleton. This is the first piece of a habit you will practice every week of this module: looking carefully at a real bone and asking what its shape and surface markings are for.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If bones were not shaped the way they are, what could you no longer do?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Bone shapes table. Make a 4-row table. For each of the four bone shapes (long, short, flat, irregular), list (a) the example you sculpted in VR, (b) a one-line description of the shape, and (c) one specific function that the shape makes possible. Be concrete — “the femur is long and narrow with expanded ends, which allows it to act as a long lever for walking” is better than “it helps the body.”
2
Bone marking analysis. In one short paragraph (3–5 sentences), describe the medial epicondyle of the humerus, the marking you labeled in VR. Classify it (projection / depression / opening), describe its location, and name what uses it.
3
Final reflection. In 3–5 sentences, answer the focus question: if bones were not shaped the way they are, what could you no longer do? Pick one specific everyday action (walking, gripping a pen, turning your head, breathing in deeply) and trace it to a specific bone shape or marking.
4
Total length: 250–400 words. Submit as Canvas text entry, or attach a .docx or .pdf. You may include or photograph the table from your notebook.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All four bone shapes named, with the correct VR example matched to each.
☐
Each shape paired with a specific function (form follows function), not a generic statement.
☐
Medial epicondyle classified as a projection and identified as a muscle attachment site.
☐
Final reflection traces one everyday action to a specific bone feature — does not restate the prompt.
