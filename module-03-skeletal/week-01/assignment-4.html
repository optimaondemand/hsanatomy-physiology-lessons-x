📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 3.04 — The Vertebral Column: Overview, Cervical, Thoracic
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 3.04 and complete the activities in Lesson 3.04, including the VR tracing session and the head-motion experiment.
Lesson focus: The spine’s 26 bones, with C1 (atlas) and C2 (axis) as the most specialized vertebrae and the 12 thoracic vertebrae paired with the 12 rib pairs.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
In this assignment you will compare C1 and C2 directly (their unique shapes and the head movements each enables), then explain the relationship between thoracic vertebrae and the ribs they articulate with. This is a form-follows-function piece: two of the most specialized bones in the human body sit at the top of the spine, and the spine’s job changes shape with every region.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How does the shape of C1, C2, and a typical thoracic vertebra each match what its region of the spine has to do?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Atlas / Axis comparison (3 column table). Build a small table with three columns: C1 (atlas), C2 (axis), typical thoracic vertebra. For each, write one row stating: (a) a distinguishing structural feature, (b) the motion the bone allows, (c) a consequence if the bone were missing.
2
Rib-to-vertebra short answer (3–5 sentences). Explain how the 12 thoracic vertebrae and the 12 pairs of ribs work together. Why do thoracic vertebrae have costal facets, and why are these features absent from cervical and lumbar vertebrae?
3
Focus-question reflection (3–5 sentences). Pick one of the three bones in your table and explain in your own words how its shape follows from its region’s job in the body.
4
Total length: 300–450 words. Submit table + responses as Canvas text entry or as a .docx/.pdf attachment.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Table contrasts C1, C2, and a typical thoracic vertebra on structure, motion, and consequence.
☐
Rib-to-vertebra answer correctly identifies costal facets and explains why they exist only on thoracic vertebrae.
☐
Focus-question reflection traces shape to function specifically — does not restate.
