🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to describe bone as living tissue and name the five major functions of the skeletal system.
2
I will be able to compare compact and spongy bone tissue and explain where each is found in a typical long bone.
3
I will be able to classify a bone as long, short, flat, or irregular and give a functional reason for the shape.

✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK
This is your first drawing session of the course. You will draw one example of each bone shape. The four bones you draw today are the first page of your Skeleton Atlas: one page per region of the skeleton, drawn and labeled by you, which you will reassemble into a complete skeleton in Week 3 for your Module 3 Assembled Figure. Keep every page — paper is highly recommended, but a digital drawing file works too.
What to do. Take a fresh page and title it “Skeleton Atlas — Page 1: Four Bone Shapes.” Use the figures in Reading 3.01 as your reference. Draw one example of each shape, large enough to label, in this order:
▶ Long bone — draw the femur. Note the narrow shaft and the expanded heads at each end.
▶ Short bone — draw any one of the carpal bones (capitate is easiest to find). Notice how it is about as wide as it is tall.
▶ Flat bone — draw the sternum. Feel how thin and broad it is compared to the femur.
▶ Irregular bone — draw L1 (the first lumbar vertebra). Notice the central body, the hole for the spinal cord, and the several projections sticking out.
Before you finish the page, add a small sketch of the lower end of the humerus and mark the medial epicondyle — the bony bump on the inside of the elbow. Label it. This is the first bone marking you will name by sight, and it will return repeatedly when we add muscles in Module 4.
