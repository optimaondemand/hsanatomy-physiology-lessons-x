Use the self-check accordion to verify you can recall the four major facial bones and the unique hyoid.
2
Self-palpation
10 min
1
Using a mirror, find each of the major facial bones on your own face: feel the mandible by opening and closing your jaw; press gently on a cheekbone (zygomatic); pinch the bridge of your nose (nasal bones); feel the upper jaw (maxilla) just under your nose. Then place a fingertip just in front of each ear and open and close your jaw slowly — you are feeling your own mandibular condyle rotate and slide inside the mandibular fossa of the temporal bone. This is your temporomandibular joint (TMJ).
2
Tilt your head back. Place a thumb and forefinger gently on either side of the front of your neck just below the jaw. Swallow. The small bone that shifts is your hyoid. Note in your notebook what it feels like to find a bone that is suspended rather than attached.
✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK
This week you draw the major facial bones plus the hyoid. The hyoid is the only bone in the body that does not articulate with another bone — pay attention to where it sits.
What to do.
▶ Take a fresh page (paper highly recommended; a digital drawing file is also fine), title it with this lesson’s region as the next page of your Skeleton Atlas, and use the figures in this week’s reading as your reference. Draw each bone large enough to label.
▶ Draw the mandible (yellow) and place a label. Find the mandibular condyle where it meets the temporal bone.
▶ Draw the maxilla (blue) and place a label.
▶ Draw the left and right nasal bones (pink/magenta).
▶ Draw the left and right zygomatic bones (green). Note the zygomatic arch.
▶ Draw the hyoid bone (pink/magenta), high in the front of the neck. Add a label that reads “hyoid — no joint.”
Bonus. On a small side sketch, draw one incisor, one canine, one premolar, and one molar in the upper jaw. Note the shape difference.
Time: ~25 minutes.
