Module thread — Courtesy. Tiny glands. Quiet hormones. The endocrine system is the body’s most polite organization — doing its work in nanograms-per-liter doses, never overstepping, never overshooting. Your snapshot is the map of how the body coordinates itself when no one is looking.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Over two weeks of this module, you have sculpted the primary endocrine glands, the dual endocrine pancreas, and a series of secondary endocrine markers onto the figure you have been building since Module 3. Because the endocrine system is unusually small and scattered, the design plan calls for the integument to remain visible during this module for spatial reference. For this assignment you reassemble all of your work into a complete endocrine map, capture the IFX snapshot, and submit it along with a short reflection caption. This is your record of having built the endocrine system across two weeks — not a perfect textbook diagram, but the figure you made.
🧸 EXPECTED STRUCTURES IN YOUR SNAPSHOT
Your snapshot must show the endocrine system on the body (with integument visible per the design plan), plus required off-body detail. Specifically:
Primary endocrine glands — on the figure
▶
Pituitary in the sella turcica, with anterior and posterior halves visibly distinguished. Infundibulum connecting it to the hypothalamus above.
▶
Thyroid gland in butterfly shape across the front of the trachea (two lobes joined by the isthmus).
▶
Four parathyroid glands on the posterior surface of the thyroid (visible when the figure is rotated to the back).
▶
Both adrenal glands on top of the kidneys, with at least one in cross-section showing the three cortical zones (glomerulosa, fasciculata, reticularis) and the medulla.
Dual endocrine — on the figure
▶
Hypothalamus visible as a region of the ventral diencephalon (transparency on the brain).
▶
Pancreas in correct position (head in the C of the duodenum, body crossing horizontally, tail toward the spleen).
Secondary endocrine markers — on the figure
▶
Light-layered shapes for: heart, kidneys, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, liver, and gonads (testes or ovaries). Each one should carry a small label naming at least one hormone it produces (e.g., heart → ANP; kidney → EPO; small intestine → GLP-1).
Off-body detail (required — at least one)
▶
An islet of Langerhans at exaggerated scale showing alpha cells (around the rim) and beta cells (in the center) and/or a labeled adrenal cross-section showing the three cortical zones and the medulla. At least one must be present.
Spatial reference
▶
Integument visible on the figure (per the Optima endocrine design plan — the endocrine glands are so small and so internal that the body surface must be shown for spatial reference).
✏️ REFLECTION CAPTION (50–100 words)
Submit a brief written caption with your IFX. Your caption should:
1. Name what you sculpted (broad regions + your off-body detail).
2. Identify one substantive thing you learned about how the endocrine system works — not just what it looks like. (Example: “Seeing the parathyroid glands tucked into the back of the thyroid made me understand why early thyroid surgeons used to accidentally remove them — and why tetany was a known post-operative complication. The size and the position make the mistake almost inevitable without the awareness of what to look for.”)
An audio caption is also acceptable in place of written, if your VR setup supports it.
