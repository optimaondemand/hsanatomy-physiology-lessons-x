Module 6 Writing Assignment
Two Speeds of Stress: Why the Body Needs Both
Analytical Essay · 30 pts · Due end of Module 6 (Week 2)
Module thread — Courtesy. Two adrenal organs, each only five grams. One acts in seconds; the other in hours. Together, with the nervous system, they run the body’s most coordinated response to disturbance — layered, polite, never overshooting. This essay asks you to explain why the coordination is necessary.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
In Module 6 you traced the body’s slow communication system from hypothalamus to peripheral target, with particular care given to the adrenal glands and the layered stress response. The fight-or-flight reaction looks instantaneous from the outside, but inside the body it is actually three pulses on three different time scales — sympathetic nerves in milliseconds, the adrenal medulla in seconds, the adrenal cortex in minutes to hours. This essay asks you to make the comparison precise, name the molecules and structures involved, and reason about why a body with only one of the three systems would not survive its environment.
✏️ THE PROMPT
Hormones act slowly compared to nerve impulses, yet some endocrine responses — like the fight-or-flight response — feel rapid. Compare how the nervous system and the endocrine system coordinate the body’s response to stress. Use the adrenal medulla and the adrenal cortex as your central case. Explain why the body needs both fast and slow control systems, and what would go wrong if it had only one.
Length: 500–700 words.
📝 WHAT A STRONG ESSAY INCLUDES
1
Open with a concrete stressor. Pick a scenario you would expect to invoke the full stress response (a near-miss in traffic; a final exam in a difficult subject; being lost on a winter trail at sundown). Sketch the scene in 1–2 sentences and tell the reader what you are about to trace.
2
The fast wave — sympathetic nerves and adrenal medulla. Distinguish the milliseconds of direct sympathetic neural action (e.g., heart rate up via nerves directly to the SA node) from the seconds-to-minutes endocrine extension via the medulla. Name the cell type that releases medullary hormones (chromaffin cells) and the two hormones (epinephrine and norepinephrine). Explain why the same molecules, when broadcast via the blood, reach tissues the nerves alone could not reach.
3
The slow wave — the HPA axis and cortisol. Trace the full cascade: hypothalamus releases CRH, anterior pituitary releases ACTH, adrenal cortex (specifically the zona fasciculata) releases cortisol. Name at least three specific effects of cortisol (gluconeogenesis in the liver, mobilization of amino acids from muscle, suppression of inflammation, mobilization of fat). Connect these effects to the requirements of sustained stress — effort lasting minutes to hours.
4
Layered, not redundant. Argue that the three time scales are not doing the same thing. The sympathetic nerves give precise speed; the medulla broadens the reach; the cortex sustains the effort. Each layer adds something the others cannot.
5
What goes wrong with only one. Use a clinical example: a patient with Addison’s disease (no cortisol) facing a major infection or injury cannot mount a sustained metabolic response and can crash into an adrenal crisis — this is the body without the slow side. Conversely, a person whose body could only release cortisol but not catecholamines would have no fast response at all and would be killed by quickly-arriving threats. Speak in concrete terms.
6
Close with the form-follows-function frame. The adrenal medulla’s embryological origin (sympathetic ganglion lineage) makes it perfect for fast hormones. The cortex’s steroid biochemistry (slow gene-expression effects) makes it perfect for sustained effort. The body’s anatomy is the explanation for the timing.
