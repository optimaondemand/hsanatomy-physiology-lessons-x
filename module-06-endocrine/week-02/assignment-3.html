📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 6.07 — Secondary Endocrine Organs
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 6.07 and complete the Lesson 6.07 activities.
Lesson focus: How do non-endocrine organs participate in the body’s chemical conversation?
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
The body’s “eleven organ systems” is a teaching scheme. The body itself does not respect the boundaries. Many organs you have learned to think of as belonging to one system — the heart, the kidneys, the gut, the liver, the gonads — are quietly part of the endocrine conversation as well. In this assignment, you will pick one secondary endocrine organ and write a short profile of how its hormone production fits into the larger picture.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why does an organ with a major non-endocrine job (digestion, filtration, pumping) also act as an endocrine organ? What does the hormone signal accomplish that the organ’s main function alone cannot?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Choose one secondary endocrine organ from this lesson’s reading:
  • Heart (ANP)
  • Kidney (EPO or renin or calcitriol — pick one)
  • Stomach or small intestine (gastrin, secretin, CCK, or GLP-1 — pick one)
  • Liver (IGF-1)
(Avoid the gonads — those will be covered in detail in Module 12.)
2
Write a 4-paragraph profile:
  • P1 — the organ’s primary, non-endocrine job. 2–3 sentences.
  • P2 — the hormone. Name the cell type or tissue region that releases it; describe what triggers its release.
  • P3 — where the hormone goes and what it does. Name target tissue(s) and the resulting change.
  • P4 — integration. How does this hormone connect this organ’s work to a larger regulatory loop? Name at least one other organ system the hormone touches.
3
Close with a short answer (2–3 sentences) to the focus question.
4
Total length: 350–450 words. Submit as a Canvas text entry, .docx, or .pdf.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA

☐

☐
