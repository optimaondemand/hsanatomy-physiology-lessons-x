✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK
What to do. On your Endocrine Map (or on a fresh page, enlarged), draw the pancreas tucked behind the stomach: the head in the curve of the duodenum, the body crossing horizontally, and the tail reaching toward the spleen. Preview where the pancreatic duct enters the duodenum — you will not need to detail it now, but mark the connection (this sets up Module 10). Then, on a separate detail page, draw an islet of Langerhans at exaggerated scale showing alpha cells (around the rim) and beta cells (in the center), with capillaries threading through.
Level of detail. The pancreas itself should be recognizable in position (behind stomach, against duodenum). The islet should be at greatly exaggerated scale — a real islet is ~100 micrometers across, so this is the same kind of zoom you used for the sarcomere in Module 4 and the synapse in Module 5.
Time: ~25 minutes.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to locate the pancreas in the abdomen and identify the islet cell types and the hormones they produce.
2
I will be able to trace what insulin and glucagon do at the level of the liver, muscle, and adipose tissue.
3
I will be able to distinguish type 1 from type 2 diabetes by mechanism and treatment.
