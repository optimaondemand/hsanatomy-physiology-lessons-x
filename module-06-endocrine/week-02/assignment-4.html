Lesson focus: What does each endocrine disorder reveal about the normal function of one piece of the system?
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
An endocrine disorder is the system telling you what it normally does, in negative. Cushing’s shows what too much cortisol breaks. Addison’s shows what no cortisol breaks. Hypothyroidism shows what every cell does at a normal metabolic rate. Diabetes shows what insulin normally accomplishes minute by minute. In this assignment you will map four named disorders to the broken machinery and the broken regulation. This is the kind of reasoning a clinician does every day.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If a clinician knows the pattern of which hormones are too high and which are too low, can they always tell which structural part of the system is broken? Where does this logic work, and where does it break down?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Build a 4-row table with the following columns: Disorder — Broken structure — Hormone(s) too high — Hormone(s) too low — Two key clinical signs.
2
Fill in for each of these four disorders:
  • Cushing’s syndrome (from a pituitary adenoma)
  • Primary hypothyroidism (Hashimoto’s)
  • Type 1 diabetes mellitus
  • Addison’s disease (primary adrenal insufficiency)
3
Below the table, write a 4–6 sentence paragraph engaging the focus question. Specifically: can a doctor always pinpoint the broken structure from blood tests alone? Where does this work cleanly (Cushing’s vs. primary adrenal Cushing-like syndrome — the upstream hormones disambiguate them)? Where does it break down (type 2 diabetes — insulin levels are not always definitive)?

Total length: a 4-row table + 4–6 sentence paragraph. Submit as a Canvas text entry plus image upload of the table, or as a .pdf with the table embedded.
