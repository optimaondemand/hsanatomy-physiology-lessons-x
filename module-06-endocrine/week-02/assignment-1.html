Reading 6.05 — The Adrenal Glands

Lesson focus: How do the adrenal medulla and adrenal cortex divide the work of the stress response across three time scales?
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
A real stress response is not a single thing. Three overlapping pulses unfold across very different time scales: a neural burst from the sympathetic nervous system, a fast endocrine wave from the adrenal medulla, and a slow sustained release of cortisol from the adrenal cortex. In this assignment you will trace all three pulses through one concrete scenario.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why is one pulse of hormones not enough? What does each layer of the stress response add that the others cannot do?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Open with a concrete scenario: a 90-minute final exam in a subject you find difficult; or a 90-minute hike up a steep, unfamiliar mountain trail. Set the scene in one sentence.
2
Write three labeled paragraphs — one for each layer of the response:
  • (A) First seconds — sympathetic neural surge. Name at least three specific changes in the body, and which sympathetic target tissues produce each one.
  • (B) Seconds to minutes — adrenal medulla. Name the cell type that releases the hormones, the two hormones, and at least two effects on tissues the sympathetic nerves could not have reached directly.
  • (C) Minutes to hours — HPA axis (cortex). Trace the cascade: hypothalamus → pituitary → adrenal cortex → cortisol. Name at least three things cortisol does to keep fuel available during sustained stress.
3
Close with a 3–5-sentence paragraph engaging the focus question. What would go wrong with your scenario if any one of the three layers were missing?
4
Total length: 400–550 words. Submit as a Canvas text entry, .docx, or .pdf.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
