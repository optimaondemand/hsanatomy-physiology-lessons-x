Reading 6.06 — The Pancreas

Before completing this assignment, read Reading 6.06 and complete the Lesson 6.06 activities.
Lesson focus: How does the pancreas defend blood glucose — and what changes when it cannot?
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
A healthy pancreas keeps blood glucose between roughly 70 and 140 mg/dL all day, every day, despite enormous swings in food intake and activity. Diabetes — in both type 1 and type 2 forms — breaks this loop in specific ways. In this assignment you will trace blood glucose across a full day in three people: a healthy person, a person with type 1 diabetes managed with insulin injections but no continuous glucose monitor, and a person with type 2 diabetes managed with diet alone. Comparing the three glucose curves makes the system’s normal job — and its specific failure modes — visible.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Type 1 and type 2 diabetes look the same on a blood test — both show high blood glucose. Why do they require different treatments?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Sketch three side-by-side line graphs of blood glucose across a 24-hour day. The x-axis is time (midnight to midnight, with breakfast at 7 am, lunch at noon, dinner at 6 pm marked). The y-axis is blood glucose in mg/dL, with the normal range (70–140) shaded.
2
Draw the blood glucose curve on each graph:
  • (A) Healthy person. Glucose rises after each meal, returns to normal within 2 hours. No excursions outside the shaded range.
  • (B) Type 1 diabetic on intermittent insulin. Glucose rises high after meals (because injection timing is imperfect), and may also dip low between meals if the insulin dose was too large. Both high and low excursions are common.
  • (C) Type 2 diabetic on diet alone. Glucose stays elevated above the shaded range most of the day, with smaller post-meal excursions; rarely drops low.
3
Below each graph, write 2–3 sentences explaining what is happening in that person’s pancreas (or where their insulin is coming from), and which target tissue is or is not responding.
4
Close with a 3–5-sentence paragraph engaging the focus question: why does it matter, for treatment, whether the problem is no insulin (T1) or resistant tissues (T2)?
5
Total length: 3 labeled graphs + ~300 words. Submit as a Canvas text entry plus image upload, or as a .pdf with the graphs embedded.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA

☐

☐
