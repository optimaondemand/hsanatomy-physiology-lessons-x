The Adrenal Glands — Stress, Fight-or-Flight, and Salt Balance
Lesson
Module thread — Courtesy. Two adrenal glands, each weighing five grams, run an emergency response that involves every organ in the body — without ever overlapping with each other’s work. The medulla goes first; the cortex sustains.

🥽 YOUR VR SESSION THIS WEEK
What to do. Open ENGAGE with your figure. Locate the kidneys in the upper retroperitoneum (you traced their position last week). Sculpt one adrenal gland on top of each kidney — small, triangular, cap-shaped. Then, on at least one of the adrenals, cut a cross-section to reveal interior structure: render the three cortical zones as concentric layers (outermost = zona glomerulosa, middle = zona fasciculata, innermost = zona reticularis) and the medulla as the inner core. Color or label them clearly. Use the “Salt, Sugar, Sex” mnemonic to remember which zone makes which hormone.
Level of detail. Both glands should be visible from in front of the body once you make the integument and abdominal organs transparent. The cross-section detail is required on one side. Add the labels for the four regions before you save.
Time: ~30 minutes.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name the three cortical zones and the main hormone made in each one, and recall the “Salt, Sugar, Sex” mnemonic.
2
I will be able to distinguish the adrenal medulla from the adrenal cortex by embryology, hormone product, control system, and speed of response.
3
I will be able to describe the three time scales of the stress response and which hormones drive each one.
