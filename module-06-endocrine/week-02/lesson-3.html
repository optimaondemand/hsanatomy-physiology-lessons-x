✏️ YOUR DRAWING SESSION THIS WEEK
What to do. This is a light layering session, not a detailed drawing session. Most of these secondary endocrine organs will be primary structures in future modules (heart in M7, intestines in M10, kidneys in M11, gonads in M12), so for this figure we will add them to your Endocrine Map as simplified reference shapes that highlight where each one releases a hormone into the body. Add: the heart (cardiovascular outline) with a small marker near the atria for ANP; both kidneys with markers for EPO, renin, and calcitriol activation; the stomach with a marker on its lining for gastrin; the small intestine with markers for secretin, CCK, GLP-1; the liver as a generic right-upper-quadrant shape with a marker for IGF-1; and the gonads (testes or ovaries) for testosterone or estrogen/progesterone.
Level of detail. Recognizable, not perfect. The point is to see all the hormone-emitting tissues together on one figure for the first time.
Time: ~20 minutes.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name at least one hormone produced by each of: heart, kidney, stomach, small intestine, liver, and gonads.
2
I will be able to explain why GLP-1 has become one of the most clinically important hormones in medicine.
3
I will be able to describe the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system at a high level — what triggers it and what it accomplishes.
