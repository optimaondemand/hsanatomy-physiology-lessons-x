The Assembled Endocrine Map
Figure submission · 25 pts · Due end of Module 6 (Week 2)
Module thread — Courtesy. Tiny glands. Quiet hormones. The endocrine system is the body’s most polite organization — doing its work in nanograms-per-liter doses, never overstepping, never overshooting. Your figure is the map of how the body coordinates itself when no one is looking.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Over two weeks of this module, you have drawn the primary endocrine glands, the dual endocrine pancreas, and a series of secondary endocrine markers on your Endocrine Map, a body outline you kept in front of you all module, with detail pages for the pituitary, thyroid and parathyroids, adrenal cross-section, and pancreatic islet. Because the endocrine system is unusually small and scattered, the body outline gives you the spatial reference for every gland. For this assignment you gather all of your work into a complete endocrine map, photograph (or export) it, and submit it along with progress images and a short reflection caption. This is your record of having built the endocrine system across two weeks — not a perfect textbook diagram, but the figure you made. Paper is highly recommended (draw by hand, lay the pages out together, photograph in good light); a digital drawing assembled on one slide is also accepted.
🧸 EXPECTED STRUCTURES IN YOUR FIGURE
Your figure must show the endocrine system on your body outline, plus required detail pages. Specifically:
Primary endocrine glands — Endocrine Map and detail pages
▶
Pituitary in the sella turcica, with anterior and posterior halves visibly distinguished. Infundibulum connecting it to the hypothalamus above.
▶
Thyroid gland in butterfly shape across the front of the trachea (two lobes joined by the isthmus).
▶
Four parathyroid glands on the posterior surface of the thyroid (shown in the posterior view).
▶
Both adrenal glands on top of the kidneys, with at least one in cross-section showing the three cortical zones (glomerulosa, fasciculata, reticularis) and the medulla.
Dual endocrine — Endocrine Map
▶
Hypothalamus drawn as a region of the ventral diencephalon on your side view of the brain.
▶
Pancreas in correct position (head in the C of the duodenum, body crossing horizontally, tail toward the spleen).
Secondary endocrine markers — Endocrine Map
▶
Light-layered shapes for: heart, kidneys, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, liver, and gonads (testes or ovaries). Each one should carry a small label naming at least one hormone it produces (e.g., heart → ANP; kidney → EPO; small intestine → GLP-1).
Detail page (required — at least one)
▶
An islet of Langerhans at exaggerated scale showing alpha cells (around the rim) and beta cells (in the center) and/or a labeled adrenal cross-section showing the three cortical zones and the medulla. At least one must be present.
Spatial reference
▶
Body outline shown on the Endocrine Map (the endocrine glands are so small and so internal that the body surface must be drawn for spatial reference).
✏️ REFLECTION CAPTION (50–100 words)
Record yourself speaking a caption of 50–100 words (about 30–50 seconds) with your phone, laptop, or the Canvas media recorder; a written caption is accepted only if you genuinely cannot record. Your caption should:
1. Name what you drew (broad regions + your detail page).
2. Identify one substantive thing you learned about how the endocrine system works — not just what it looks like. (Example: “Seeing the parathyroid glands tucked into the back of the thyroid made me understand why early thyroid surgeons used to accidentally remove them — and why tetany was a known post-operative complication. The size and the position make the mistake almost inevitable without the awareness of what to look for.”)
Attach two dated progress images — one page from each week (for example, from Lessons 6.03 and 6.06).
