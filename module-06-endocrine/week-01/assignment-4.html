Reading 6.04 — The Thyroid and Parathyroid Glands
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 6.04 and complete the Lesson 6.04 activities.
Lesson focus: How does the body hold blood calcium in a narrow range every minute of every day?
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Blood calcium has to stay between roughly 8.5 and 10.5 mg/dL or nerves and muscles stop firing properly. Four tiny parathyroid glands — each the size of a grain of rice — carry the entire job. In this assignment you will trace step-by-step what happens when blood calcium drops, naming every anatomical structure and every hormone the signal touches.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why does a single calcium-regulating hormone (PTH) need to act on three different organs to do its job — rather than just one?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Open with one sentence: a scenario in which blood calcium drops (e.g., a person who has been on a calcium-poor diet for weeks; or a patient whose parathyroid glands have been accidentally removed during a thyroid surgery).
2
Walk through the calcium-restoring response in four numbered steps:
  • Step 1 — sensing. What detects the drop in blood calcium? Name the gland and explain how it monitors the bloodstream directly (no pituitary required).
  • Step 2 — bone. How does PTH act on bone? Name the cell type that responds and describe what is released into the blood.
  • Step 3 — kidney. Two effects: calcium handling and phosphate handling, and the activation of vitamin D. Be specific about the direction of each.
  • Step 4 — gut. How does active vitamin D (calcitriol) help the small intestine absorb more dietary calcium?
3
Close with a paragraph (3–5 sentences) that answers the focus question. Why is it not enough for PTH to just “tell the bone” to release calcium? What does each of the other two organs add that bone alone cannot do?
4
Choose whichever format walks through the four steps most clearly for you: (a) the written version above (300–450 words); (b) a recorded 3–4 minute video or audio walkthrough narrating the four steps in order; or (c) a labeled flowchart/circuit diagram of the four steps plus 1–2 sentences of written explanation per step. All three formats are graded against the same rubric below.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
