Lesson 6.03
The Hypothalamus and the Pituitary Gland
Lesson
Module thread — Courtesy. The hypothalamus is the body’s most polite organ. It does not shout commands. It releases nanograms of releasing hormone into a tiny portal vein and trusts the pituitary to listen. Notice how much of the body runs on this kind of quiet authority.
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Reading 6.03 — The Hypothalamus and the Pituitary Gland
The substantive content for this lesson lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before you start the activities below, open Reading 6.03 in Canvas and read it end-to-end. Be ready to recite the six anterior pituitary hormones from memory before the VR session.
🥽 YOUR VR SESSION THIS WEEK
What to do. Open ENGAGE with your figure (integument on, brain visible via transparency). Locate the hypothalamus — the floor of the diencephalon, just above the sella turcica. Sculpt the hypothalamus as a small region in the inferior diencephalon. Then sculpt the infundibulum — the stalk that hangs down from it — and the pituitary gland nestled in the sella turcica of the sphenoid bone (use your Module 3 skeleton if helpful). Render the pituitary in two colors or two regions: the anterior pituitary (about two-thirds of the gland, front and lateral) and the posterior pituitary (the smaller back portion, continuous with the infundibulum). Label both halves and the infundibulum.
Level of detail. This is your first sculpted endocrine structure. Aim for recognizable, not perfect. The goal is to demonstrate relative location and anatomical relationships — where the hypothalamus, infundibulum, and pituitary sit with respect to each other — not precise anatomical shape or scale. Do not spend excessive time trying to produce an anatomically exact sculpture; the pituitary should be visibly pea-sized, and the hypothalamus should be small enough that you can see it is a brain region, not a separate organ.
Time: ~30 minutes.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name the six anterior pituitary hormones and the two posterior pituitary hormones, and identify which gland each one came from originally.
2
I will be able to trace one hypothalamic-pituitary-target axis (HPT, HPA, or HPG) end-to-end and show the negative-feedback step.
3
I will be able to explain anatomically why the anterior and posterior pituitary are different organs even though they sit together in the sella turcica.
