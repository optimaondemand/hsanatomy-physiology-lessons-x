Lesson 6.04
The Thyroid and Parathyroid Glands
Lesson
Module thread — Courtesy. Four tiny parathyroid glands — each about the size of a grain of rice — quietly hold your blood calcium steady. Your nerves and muscles depend on it. Notice how often the body’s most life-critical jobs are done by its smallest tenants.

🥽 YOUR VR SESSION THIS WEEK
What to do. Open ENGAGE with your figure (integument on). Make the integument over the neck partially transparent. Sculpt the thyroid gland across the front of the trachea, just inferior to the larynx. Render it as a butterfly: two lateral lobes joined by a thin isthmus across the trachea. Then rotate the figure to view the thyroid from behind, and place four parathyroid glands on the posterior surface of the thyroid — two pairs, one set on each lobe. Each parathyroid should be visibly tiny (grain-of-rice scale) compared to the thyroid itself.
Level of detail. The butterfly shape of the thyroid should be recognizable from the front. The parathyroids should be visible only when you rotate to the back. This is the spatial fact that matters most: anatomical neighbors, completely different jobs.
Time: ~25 minutes.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to locate the thyroid and parathyroid glands on a figure and name the hormones each produces.
2
I will be able to trace the calcium-homeostasis loop: low blood calcium → PTH → bone, kidney, gut → calcium back to normal.
3
I will be able to explain why TSH and T4 change in opposite directions when the thyroid is diseased.

Calcium homeostasis circuit
15 min
1
In your notebook, draw a single page with blood calcium in the center. From it, draw two arrows out: one for “too low” and one for “too high.”
2
For “too low,” follow PTH out to bone (osteoclasts ↑), kidney (Ca reabsorption ↑, phosphate excretion ↑), and gut (via active vitamin D, Ca absorption ↑). All three loops bring calcium up.
3
For “too high,” show PTH falling and calcitonin nudging calcium back down (small effect in adults but worth labeling).
