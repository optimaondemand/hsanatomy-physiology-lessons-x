📖 READING FOCUS
Reading 6.01 — Endocrine vs. Exocrine: What Is a Hormone?
The substantive content for this assignment lives on the paired Reading page in Canvas.
Before completing this assignment, read Reading 6.01 (and complete the activities in Lesson 6.01 if you have not yet).
Lesson focus: What is a hormone, and how does its chemistry decide how it works?
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
A hormone’s chemistry determines everything about how it works at its target cell: whether it can cross the membrane, where its receptor sits, how fast it acts, and how long its effect lasts. In this assignment you will classify five hormones into their families, identify where each one’s receptor is located, and predict the speed and duration of its action. Then you will explain why one of these hormones acts the way it does, in your own words.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If you know nothing about a hormone except its chemical family, what can you already predict about how it works?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Re-read the “What is a hormone?” and “How a hormone changes a cell” sections of Reading 6.01.
2
Build a table with five rows (one for each hormone below) and four columns: Hormone family (peptide / steroid / catecholamine / thyroid), Receptor location (cell surface / cytoplasm or nucleus), Speed of onset (seconds / minutes / hours), Duration (brief / sustained).
3
Fill the table for: (a) insulin, (b) cortisol, (c) epinephrine, (d) thyroxine (T4), (e) growth hormone. For receptor location, speed, and duration, justify each cell with one short phrase (not a full sentence) tied to the hormone’s chemistry.
4
In a final paragraph (4–6 sentences), engage the focus question: pick one of the five hormones and explain why a cell biologist who knew only its chemical family would already be able to predict its receptor location, speed, and duration. Do not restate the question — answer it.
5
Total length: a clear 5-row table + 4–6 sentence paragraph. Submit as a Canvas text entry, a .docx, or a .pdf.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
