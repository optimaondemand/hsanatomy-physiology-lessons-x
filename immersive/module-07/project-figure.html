The Assignment

Over three weeks you have drawn blood (the formed elements at exaggerated scale), the heart (outside and inside, with the conduction system and the coronary tree), and the great arterial and venous trees on your Circulatory Map, a large front-view body outline. For this assignment you gather all of that work into one complete view, photograph or export it, and submit it with progress images and a short spoken caption. This is the record of having built the cardiovascular system over three weeks: not a perfect textbook diagram, but the figure you actually made.

Paper or Digital

Paper is highly recommended: draw by hand, lay the pages out together, and photograph in good light (a wide shot plus one or two close-ups). A digital drawing assembled on one slide and exported as an image or PDF is also accepted.

A

Expected Structures

Tick each item when it is drawn and labeled.

  • Heart in position: in the mediastinum, apex down and to the left, with the pericardium as a dashed outline.
  • External anatomy: both auricles visible; four chambers in their correct arrangement.
  • Six great vessels: SVC, IVC, pulmonary trunk, four pulmonary veins, aorta, correctly attached and color-coded (blue deoxygenated path, red oxygenated).
  • Internal heart (front-cut section): four chambers, four valves with visible cusps, the interventricular septum, chordae tendineae on the AV valves.
  • Conduction system: in a distinct color (yellow): SA node, AV node, bundle of His, bundle branches, Purkinje fibers.
  • Coronary tree: LAD, circumflex and right coronary artery, plus the great cardiac vein.
  • Aorta (Circulatory Map): ascending, arch, thoracic, abdominal, with the three arch branches in order: brachiocephalic, left common carotid, left subclavian.
  • Head and neck: common carotids (internal + external) and vertebral arteries.
  • Both arms: subclavian → axillary → brachial → radial + ulnar.
  • Abdominal branches: celiac, superior mesenteric, both renals, inferior mesenteric.
  • Both legs: common iliac → external iliac → femoral → popliteal → anterior tibial, posterior tibial, fibular.
  • Major veins: internal and external jugulars, brachiocephalics → SVC; subclavian, axillary, basilic, cephalic, median cubital; common iliacs → IVC; femoral, popliteal, great saphenous.
  • Hepatic portal vein: a separate detour from the gut to the liver.

The Cellular Detail Page

The red cell and the five leukocytes are required; the fetal-shunt page is optional but encouraged.

  • One biconcave red blood cell (Lesson 7.02): at exaggerated scale.
  • The five leukocytes (Lesson 7.03): neutrophil, lymphocyte, monocyte, eosinophil, basophil, in a row beside the red cell for scale.
  • Optional: the fetal-shunt diagram (Lesson 7.12): ductus venosus, foramen ovale, ductus arteriosus and the umbilical vessels.

Reflection Caption (75–125 words)

Record yourself speaking a caption of 75–125 words (about 45–60 seconds) with your phone, laptop, or the Canvas media recorder. A written caption is accepted only if you genuinely cannot record.

The prompt. (1) Name what you drew: the broad regions and your detail page. (2) Identify one substantive thing you learned about how the cardiovascular system works, not just what it looks like. For example: “Tracing the radial artery from the wrist all the way back to the aortic arch made me see why a pulse at the wrist is a real measurement of the left ventricle: the blood-pressure cuff is sampling the same wave one step closer to the heart.”

Record it here, then download the recording and upload it to the Canvas assignment.
